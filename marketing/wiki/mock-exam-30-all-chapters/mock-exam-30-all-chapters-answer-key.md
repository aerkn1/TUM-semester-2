## Question-By-Question Explanations

### Chapter 01

**Q1 - Answer C**

Segmentation divides the market into meaningful groups. Targeting is the later managerial choice of which segment to serve. A segment can be identifiable, but still unattractive because it is unprofitable, unreachable, too small, or strategically wrong.

**Q2 - Answer D**

Recruitment, retention, and recovery are the 3Rs. Recovery can restore trust after a failure, but it does not always produce stronger loyalty than if no failure occurred. Do not overclaim the service recovery paradox.

**Q3 - Answer C**

Statements 1, 3, and 4 are correct. Lifestyle and personality are psychographic, not demographic. Demographic examples include age, income, education, family status, gender, or occupation.

**Q4 - Answer B**

Satisfaction supports retention, but does not guarantee loyalty. Customers may still switch because of price, convenience, variety seeking, lock-in fatigue, better alternatives, or changed needs.

**Q5 - Answer D**

All four are correct. Search properties can be judged before purchase, experience properties after use, and trust properties remain difficult to verify. Specialty goods often involve high commitment, identity, or strong preference.

### Chapter 02

**Q6 - Answer D**

All four are correct. Keep the sequence clean: brand identity is intended by the firm, brand image is perceived by customers, brand knowledge is awareness plus image, and CBBE is the differential consumer response caused by that brand knowledge.

**Q7 - Answer D**

A brand first needs points of parity to be considered legitimate in the category. Points of difference then explain why this brand should be chosen. Being different without meeting category expectations can make the brand irrelevant or non-credible.

**Q8 - Answer A**

Keller's CBBE pyramid moves from salience to performance/imagery, then judgments/feelings, then resonance. The managerial logic is memory -> meaning -> response -> relationship.

**Q9 - Answer D**

Celebrity endorsement can increase attention and transfer associations, but it can also create poor-fit effects, scandal spillover, overexposure, or dilution.

**Q10 - Answer D**

High brand recall indicates salience/awareness. Emotional brand relationship requires stronger evidence such as loyalty, attachment, community, engagement, or resonance.

### Chapter 03

**Q11 - Answer A**

The core product is the fundamental benefit or problem solution. A drill's core benefit is the hole/problem solution, not the physical drill.

**Q12 - Answer C**

Product elimination should consider contribution, fixed cost coverage, brand image, assortment role, customer relationships, supplier effects, know-how, and strategic fit, not only current unit sales.

**Q13 - Answer C**

Statements 1, 2, and 4 are correct. Recommender accuracy is not enough because privacy, fairness, accountability, transparency, explainability, and creepiness concerns affect trust and acceptance.

**Q14 - Answer B**

Statements 1, 2, and 3 are correct. Co-creation can be valuable, but it can also create expectation problems, unfeasible ideas, incrementalism, overload, or selection issues.

**Q15 - Answer D**

Waterfall fits stable requirements and stronger upfront planning. Agile or lean startup logic fits uncertain customer needs, evolving technology, and situations where feedback reduces risk.

### Chapter 04

**Q16 - Answer B**

```text
Contribution per unit = p - c_var = 80 - 30 = EUR 50
x_crit = C_fix / contribution per unit = 40,000 / 50 = 800 units
```

Interpretation: the firm must sell 800 units to cover fixed costs before profit begins.

**Q17 - Answer D**

```text
CMR = (p - c_var) / p
CMR = (80 - 30) / 80 = 50 / 80 = 0.625 = 62.5%
```

Interpretation: each sales euro leaves 62.5 cents for fixed cost and profit.

**Q18 - Answer B**

Dynamic pricing can be economically rational but behaviorally rejected. Customers compare prices to reference points and judge fairness, especially when price changes seem exploitative or opaque.

**Q19 - Answer D**

All four support price differentiation. The firm needs heterogeneous WTP, a segmentation or self-selection mechanism, limited arbitrage, and a fairness/complexity check.

**Q20 - Answer C**

```text
MR = MC
5 - 2x/3000 = 1
4 = 2x/3000
12,000 = 2x
x = 6,000 units
```

The `2x` comes from differentiating `x^2`; it is not an arbitrary doubling.

### Chapter 05

**Q21 - Answer C**

Statements 1, 2, and 3 are correct. Measurement includes pre-tests, post-tests, recall, recognition, attitudes, behavior, target fit, and other KPI layers. It is not only final sales revenue.

**Q22 - Answer B**

```text
Raw CPT = 50,000 / (2,000,000 / 1,000)
Raw CPT = 50,000 / 2,000 = EUR 25

Weighted CPT = raw CPT / target-group share
Weighted CPT = 25 / 0.50 = EUR 50
```

Interpretation: the campaign looks cheap in raw reach, but target correction doubles the effective cost per thousand target contacts.

**Q23 - Answer D**

Persuasion knowledge means consumers recognize persuasion attempts and cope. Coping can include rejection, acceptance, negotiation, ignoring, or strategic use. It does not always produce rejection.

**Q24 - Answer C**

Statements 1, 2, and 3 are correct. Reach means opportunity for exposure. It does not prove attention, understanding, attitude change, or purchase.

**Q25 - Answer A**

```text
GRPs = reach percentage * average frequency
GRPs = 40 * 3 = 120
```

Use GRPs for contact pressure, not unique reach.

### Chapter 06

**Q26 - Answer D**

Not every intermediary is a retailer. Merchants buy and resell, agents negotiate or search without title, and facilitators support distribution without buying or selling.

**Q27 - Answer C**

Statements 1, 2, and 4 are correct. Omnichannel strategy integrates online and offline channels into one customer journey. It does not intentionally separate them.

**Q28 - Answer D**

Maximum outlet coverage is not always best. Intensive distribution fits many convenience goods, while selective or exclusive distribution may fit image-sensitive, service-heavy, luxury, or scarcity-based offerings.

**Q29 - Answer A**

Statements 1, 2, and 4 are correct. Direct-to-consumer moves and private labels can increase conflict. Joint objectives and aligned incentives can mitigate conflict. Exclusive distribution improves control but does not automatically align all goals.

**Q30 - Answer C**

Statements 1, 2, and 3 are correct. AI personalization can scale relevance, but it may also create privacy, trust, creepiness, or service-quality problems. Human-enabled personalization can be superior when empathy, judgment, and trust matter.
