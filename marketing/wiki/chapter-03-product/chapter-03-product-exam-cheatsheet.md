# Chapter 03: Product - Exam Cheatsheet

Source note: [chapter-03-product.md](chapter-03-product.md)

Created: 2026-07-15

Updated: 2026-07-22 - aligned with mock/example exam product, AI, co-creation, and innovation-management traps.

Exam context: Marketing exam on 2026-07-22 is a 90-minute written exam. Product questions can be conceptual, applied, or research-method oriented. This sheet does not mark `First Pass` as completed.

## Core Exam Move

```text
Customer problem -> product level -> value mechanism -> product decision
-> customer/firm tradeoff -> exam trap.
```

Start with the benefit, not the object. The drill example means customers do not buy a drill as such; they buy the hole/problem solution.

## Product Levels

| Level | Meaning | Hotel example | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core product | fundamental benefit | rest and sleep | not the physical room |
| Generic product | basic product form | room, bed, bathroom | minimum viable form |
| Expected product | must-have standards | clean bed, towels | failure here destroys satisfaction |
| Augmented product | extra features | high service, free Wi-Fi | cannot compensate for missing basics |
| Potential product | future evolution | smart room, digital concierge | innovation direction |

Exam sentence:

```text
Customers evaluate the whole benefit bundle: core solution, expected basics, augmentation, brand, service, packaging, and future potential.
```

## Product Decision Router

| Question signal | Use this concept | What to answer |
|---|---|---|
| Physical product becomes digital | transformation | lower marginal distribution cost, platform dependence, new pricing |
| Physical product gains software/data | augmentation | service platform, data, lock-in, privacy/cybersecurity |
| Packaging example | packaging functions | protection, brand, sales, logistics, use, sustainability, augmentation |
| Many product categories | product mix breadth | number of product lines/groups |
| Many variants inside one line | product mix depth | number of articles in a line |
| Declining product | elimination decision | profitability plus image, fixed costs, know-how, suppliers/customers |
| Recommendation system | collaborative/content-based filtering | accuracy plus FATE and privacy |
| New-product uncertainty | agile/lean startup | test assumptions iteratively |
| Preference measurement | conjoint | tradeoffs among attribute levels |

## Digitalization And AI

| Concept | Core idea | Exam risk |
|---|---|---|
| Physical-to-digital transformation | Product becomes digital, such as CDs to streaming. | ownership and platform dependence change. |
| Physical augmentation | Digital elements enhance a physical product. | service/data opportunities plus privacy and security. |
| Collaborative filtering | similar users guide recommendations. | can reinforce bias or popularity. |
| Content-based filtering | item attributes guide recommendations. | can narrow discovery. |
| FATE | fairness, accountability, transparency, explainability. | trust matters beyond accuracy. |
| Creepiness/privacy concern | personalization exceeds expected data use. | relevance can become invasive. |

## Innovation And Development

Stage-gate sequence:

```text
Idea generation -> screening -> concept development/testing
-> marketing strategy -> business analysis -> product development
-> market testing -> commercialization
```

Technology push vs market pull:

| Logic | Starts from | Strength | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technology push | technical/R&D possibilities | radical solutions customers did not imagine | missing real needs |
| Market pull | expressed market need | better market fit | incrementalism |

Lead users:

```text
Lead users experience future mainstream needs earlier and may already develop solutions.
```

Co-creation:

```text
Useful when firms can manage expectations, select ideas, and turn customer input into feasible offerings.
Not automatically successful.
```

## Conjoint Analysis

Purpose:

```text
Estimate how customers value product attributes and levels by forcing tradeoffs.
```

Simple utility language:

```text
Total utility = part-worth(attribute level A) + part-worth(attribute level B) + ...
```

Choice-based conjoint:

```text
Respondents repeatedly choose between product concepts, closer to marketplace choice and able to include a no-choice option.
```

Trap:

```text
Too many attributes create overload; too few miss relevant tradeoffs.
```

## Written-Exam Answer Skeletons

Product-level case:

```text
1. Identify the core benefit.
2. Separate generic and expected basics.
3. Add augmentation and potential evolution.
4. Explain which level drives satisfaction or differentiation.
```

Product elimination case:

```text
Declining metric -> possible elimination -> check fixed-cost coverage,
brand/image role, relationships, know-how, suppliers, and customer effects.
```

AI recommender case:

```text
Recommendation logic -> customer value -> FATE/privacy concern
-> trust safeguard -> managerial decision.
```

## Mock And Example Exam Upgrade

Product MCQs often ask which statement is **incorrect**. Start by deciding whether the statement is about the benefit, the physical object, the product line, or the innovation process.

Option-elimination table:

| Exam signal | Correct answer logic | Reject options that say |
|---|---|---|
| Core product | Fundamental benefit or problem solution. | The core product is the physical object. |
| Expected product | Must-have baseline standards. | Augmentation can compensate for missing basics. |
| Product mix breadth/depth | Breadth = number of product lines; depth = variants inside a line. | Breadth and depth are interchangeable. |
| Product elimination | Consider contribution, fixed-cost coverage, brand image, know-how, suppliers, customers, and strategic role. | Eliminate based only on current unit sales. |
| Packaging | Protects, communicates, sells, supports logistics/use, and signals sustainability. | Packaging is only protection or independent of environmental expectations. |
| Recommenders | Collaborative = similar users/user-item patterns; content-based = item attributes. | Accuracy alone is enough for a good recommender decision. |
| Co-creation | Useful input, but must be selected, filtered, tested, and implemented. | Customer ideas are automatically successful or final design. |
| Lead users | Users who experience future mainstream needs early. | Every average customer is a lead user. |
| Conjoint | Estimates attribute tradeoffs; too many attributes overload respondents. | Conjoint directly proves total market success. |

Innovation-management bridge from the example exam:

| Concept | Fast distinction | Exam trap |
|---|---|---|
| Technology push | Starts from R&D or technical possibilities. | Ignoring real customer need. |
| Market pull | Starts from expressed market need. | Can become incremental. |
| Agile | Iterative, feedback-driven, useful under uncertain requirements. | Agile means no planning/documentation. |
| Waterfall | Linear, upfront-planned, useful when requirements are stable. | Waterfall is best when needs and technology are unclear. |
| Product innovation | Changes the customer-facing offering, such as new recipes. | Calling every new recipe a paradigm innovation. |
| Process innovation | Changes how the offering is produced or delivered, such as backend logistics. | Reversing product and process innovation. |
| Delphi | Anonymous, multi-round expert forecasting. | One expert or dominant-personality panel. |
| Trend extrapolation | Projects stable past trends into the future. | Reliable under discontinuity. |
| Tacit knowledge | Transferred through mentorship, shadowing, and informal networks. | Fully captured by manuals or dashboards. |
| Innovation leadership | Enables experimentation, curiosity, psychological safety, and exploration/exploitation. | Hero leader solves every technical problem or tight top-down control is best. |

## Traps And Corrections

| Trap | Correction |
|---|---|
| Product = physical item. | Product is a benefit bundle and problem solution. |
| Delighters fix missing basics. | Expected-product failures must be solved first. |
| Packaging = protection only. | Packaging also communicates, sells, enables use, and augments the product. |
| Breadth = depth. | Breadth is number of lines; depth is variants within a line. |
| Co-creation always improves innovation. | It can create incremental ideas, overload, and expectation problems. |
| AI accuracy is enough. | Fairness, accountability, transparency, explainability, and privacy shape trust. |

## Last Line

```text
Product policy is the design of a customer problem solution, not just management of a physical object.
```
