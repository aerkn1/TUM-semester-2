# Chapter 02: Branding - Exam Cheatsheet

Source note: [chapter-02-branding.md](chapter-02-branding.md)

Created: 2026-07-15

Exam context: Marketing exam on 2026-07-22 is a 90-minute written exam with special focus on discussed research papers. This sheet is for rapid applied recall and does not mark `First Pass` as completed.

## Core Exam Move

```text
Target customer -> category frame -> points of parity -> points of difference
-> brand knowledge -> strong/favorable/unique associations
-> customer response -> equity risk.
```

For branding questions, do not start with the logo. Start with what the brand changes in the customer's mind and behavior.

## Must-Know Definitions

| Concept | Exam-safe wording | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Brand | Name, term, sign, symbol, design, or combination that identifies and differentiates a seller's offer. | Not only a logo. |
| Product | Offering that satisfies a need or want. | Brand and product are not identical. |
| Brand identity | Intended meaning from firm perspective. | Not fully controlled. |
| Brand image | Actual consumer perception. | Can diverge from identity. |
| Brand knowledge | Brand awareness plus brand image. | More than recall. |
| Brand equity | Added value endowed by the brand. | Not just financial valuation. |
| CBBE | Differential effect of brand knowledge on consumer response to marketing. | Must link to consumer mind. |

## Association Rule

```text
Strategically valuable brand associations are strong, favorable, and unique.
```

| Dimension | Ask | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Strength | How firmly is it linked in memory? | Apple -> design. |
| Favorability | Does it satisfy needs/wants? | Volvo -> safety. |
| Uniqueness | Does it distinguish the brand? | Rolex -> prestige and heritage. |

## Positioning Router

| Positioning element | Meaning | Exam use |
|---|---|---|
| Frame of reference | Category in which the brand competes. | Shows what customers compare it against. |
| Points of parity | Associations needed for category legitimacy. | "Good enough to be considered." |
| Points of difference | Unique, favorable, strong reasons to choose. | "Why this brand?" |
| Brand mantra | Short internal essence of positioning. | Guides product, communication, place, and price decisions. |

Exam sentence:

```text
A brand cannot only be different; it must first meet category expectations and then create a valued difference.
```

## Keller CBBE Pyramid

```text
Salience -> Performance / Imagery -> Judgments / Feelings -> Resonance
```

| Level | Customer question | What to mention |
|---|---|---|
| Salience | Who are you? | recall, recognition, usage situations |
| Performance | What are you? | quality, reliability, utilitarian value |
| Imagery | What do you mean? | user profile, values, heritage, experiences |
| Judgments | What do I think? | quality, credibility, superiority |
| Feelings | What do I feel? | emotions and social currency |
| Resonance | What relationship do we have? | loyalty, attachment, community, engagement |

## Brand Elements Checklist

```text
Memorable, meaningful, likable, transferable, adaptable, protectable.
```

Offensive criteria build equity: memorable, meaningful, likable.

Defensive criteria preserve and leverage equity: transferable, adaptable, protectable.

## Architecture And Leverage

| Strategy | Existing/new brand | Existing/new product line | Example logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Line extension | existing brand | existing line | Coca-Cola Zero. |
| Brand extension | existing brand | new line | Virgin into airlines. |
| Multi-branding | new brand | existing category | multiple detergent brands for segments. |
| New brand/new line | new brand | new line | when fit is weak or dilution risk is high. |

| Leveraging route | Benefit | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Co-branding | transfer associations from two brands | poor fit or confusion |
| Ingredient branding | quality signal for a component | dependency or weak visibility |
| Licensing | quick access to known associations | overexposure and dilution |
| Celebrity endorsement | attention and association transfer | bad fit or negative spillover |

Write it like this if asked:

The paper examines whether and how slow-motion presentation can increase
perceived product luxury. Practically, it shows that execution style can

## Traps And Corrections

| Trap | Correction |
|---|---|
| Brand = logo. | Brand includes associations, meaning, risk reduction, and customer response. |
| Identity = image. | Identity is intended; image is perceived. |
| Differentiation alone is enough. | Add points of parity first. |
| Strong brand extension is always good. | Check fit, dilution risk, and category credibility. |
| Celebrity visibility proves effectiveness. | Fit, credibility, and association transfer matter. |
| Brand equity is only firm-side. | CBBE starts in consumers' minds and hearts. |

## Last Line

```text
Brand equity exists when brand knowledge changes how customers perceive, feel, choose, pay, and stay.
```
