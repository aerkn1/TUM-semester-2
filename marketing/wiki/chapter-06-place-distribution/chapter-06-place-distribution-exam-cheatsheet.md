# Chapter 06: Place And Distribution - Exam Cheatsheet

Source note: [chapter-06-place-distribution.md](chapter-06-place-distribution.md)

Created: 2026-07-15

Exam context: Marketing exam on 2026-07-22 is a 90-minute written exam and may test research-paper slides. This sheet does not mark `First Pass` as completed.

## Core Exam Move

```text
Customer access need -> route/directness -> channel actors
-> coverage intensity -> control/margin/data/service/conflict
-> retail/research implication.
```

Place is not only shipping. It decides how value reaches the customer, who controls the relationship, and what experience/data the firm gets back.

## Channel Actor Definitions

| Actor | Role | Takes title? | Example |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Merchant | buys and resells merchandise | yes | wholesaler, retailer |
| Agent | searches, negotiates, or represents producer | no | broker, sales agent |
| Facilitator | supports distribution without buying/negotiating | no | transport, warehouse, bank |

Exam trap:

```text
Not every intermediary is a retailer. Only merchants take title and resell.
```

## Distribution Decision Router

| Question signal | Use this concept | Answer angle |
|---|---|---|
| Who sells/controls customer relation? | distribution management | governance, contracts, sales bodies, channel control |
| Physical delivery/warehouse flow | logistics | movement and information flow |
| Producer sells without intermediary | direct distribution | control, margin, data, capability burden |
| Producer uses intermediaries | indirect distribution | reach, specialization, dependency, conflict |
| How many layers? | vertical structure | direct, one-stage, two-stage, several-stage |
| How many/type of partners per layer? | horizontal structure | breadth and depth |
| Producer moves toward retail | forward integration | factory outlet, own store |
| Retailer moves toward production | backward integration | private label |

## Push, Pull, And Coverage

| Choice | Core logic | Best fit | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Push | producer motivates intermediaries | trade promotions, retailer incentives | depends on channel cooperation |
| Pull | final-customer demand pressures channel | brand advertising, consumer demand | requires customer preference |
| Intensive selling | broad availability | convenience goods/FMCG | reach high, control low |
| Selective selling | choose retailers by criteria | service/image-sensitive products | balanced reach/control |
| Exclusive selling | very few partners | luxury/scarcity/high service | high control, low reach |

Coverage correction:

```text
Maximum coverage is not automatically best. Match coverage to customer need, product type, service requirement, brand image, and control need.
```

## Channel Conflict

| Area | Manufacturer tendency | Retailer tendency |
|---|---|---|
| Product | protect brand/product image | protect assortment/private label |
| Distribution | broad coverage, large deliveries | selective focus, just-in-time |
| Communication | brand loyalty | retailer loyalty |
| Price | consistent prices, low retailer margin | differentiated prices, higher margin |

Exam sentence:

```text
Channel conflict arises because independent actors optimize different goals, margins, information, and expectations.
```

## Research-Slide Watchlist

| Research topic | What to remember | Exam interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Physical-store personalization | human-enabled vs technology-enabled personalization | scale/data versus empathy/trust |
| In-store tracking methods | surveys, observation, RFID, CCTV, eye tracking, VR, field eye tracking | each method measures a different layer |
| Metaverse retail | playfulness and connectedness -> self-expansion -> virtual-item purchase intention | virtual retail is identity/social experience, not just a digital shelf |
| Consumer haptics | actual, device-mediated, imaginal, and language-evoked touch | touch can matter without physical contact |

Method trap:

```text
Eye tracking measures attention. It does not prove purchase intention or causality alone.
```

## Written-Exam Answer Skeletons

Channel recommendation case:

```text
1. State distribution objective and customer access need.
2. Choose direct/indirect and channel stages.
3. Identify merchants, agents, and facilitators.
4. Choose intensive/selective/exclusive coverage.
5. Discuss control, margin, data, service, image, and conflict.
6. Link to product, price, promotion, and customer experience.
```

Luxury brand case:

```text
Prefer selective/exclusive coverage when service quality, scarcity,
presentation, and brand image matter more than ubiquity.
```

FMCG case:

```text
Prefer intensive distribution because convenience goods need availability,
habit cues, and low search effort.
```

Retail technology case:

```text
Technology -> friction reduction/personalization/data
-> customer value -> privacy/trust/service-quality risk
-> safeguard or hybrid human-tech design.
```

## Traps And Corrections

| Trap | Correction |
|---|---|
| Distribution = logistics. | Distribution includes channel governance plus physical/logistical flow. |
| More outlets is always better. | Coverage must fit product, image, service, and control needs. |
| Push means aggressive tone. | Push targets intermediaries; pull targets final customers. |
| Every third party is a retailer. | Merchant, agent, and facilitator are different. |
| AI personalization is always superior. | Compare scalability with privacy, trust, and human empathy. |
| Metaverse store = online shop. | It can create playfulness, connectedness, and self-expansion. |
| Touch means physical touch only. | Haptics can be device-mediated, imagined, or language-based. |

## Last Line

```text
Place answer = access + channel structure + actor roles + coverage + control/conflict + customer experience.
```
