## Prompts, User Answers, And Feedback

### Case 1: Online Headphones, No Defect

Prompt:

> A student buys headphones from a trader's online shop. The headphones arrive on time and work perfectly, but the student dislikes the color and wants to send them back. Is this rescission, revocation, withdrawal, cancellation, or dissolution? Why is it not Contract Law II revocation? Which statutory anchors would you start with?

User answer:

> withdrawal; because no breach or performance issue in contract declaration or agreement. Relevant to consumer preference. consumer under section13?

Feedback:

- Correct route: **withdrawal**.
- Tight wording: not revocation because there is no performance breach in a valid reciprocal contract.
- Full statutory route: Sections 13, 14, 312c, 312g, 355, and 357 BGB.

Quality: `green`

### Case 2: Defective Machine

Prompt:

> A company buys a machine from another company. The machine is delivered, but it does not work as promised. The buyer wants to get out of the contract. Is this withdrawal, revocation, cancellation, rescission, or dissolution? Why?

User answer:

> revocation, its relevant to the performance after contract and shipping is made

Feedback:

- Correct route: **revocation**.
- Wording repair: say **delivery/performance**, not shipping.
- Main anchor: Section 323 BGB, plus declaration under Section 349 BGB and restitution under Sections 346-348 BGB.

Quality: `green`

### Case 3: Gym Membership Access Blocked

Prompt:

> A consumer signs a contract for a gym membership for 24 months. After 6 months, the gym repeatedly blocks access to the facilities without justification and ignores complaints. The consumer wants to end the relationship going forward. Is this withdrawal, revocation, cancellation, rescission, or dissolution? Why?

User answer:

> it sounds cancellation, violence of contiuing obligation

Feedback:

- Correct route: **cancellation**.
- Wording repair: say **breach/violation of a continuing obligation**, not "violence."
- Reason: gym membership is a continuing obligation and the legal issue is ending the relationship for the future.
- Main anchor: Section 314 BGB; Section 241 II BGB may support the duty-breach/trust-collapse analysis.

Quality: `yellow-green`

### Case 4: Mutual Startup Exit Agreement

Prompt:

> A startup founder and an investor have a financing/cooperation agreement. The relationship becomes tense. Instead of relying on a unilateral legal right, they both sign a written agreement ending the cooperation and settling payments. Which route is this: withdrawal, revocation, cancellation, rescission, or dissolution? Why?

User answer:

> dissoluton, both side agrees to terminate the contract

Feedback:

- Correct route: **dissolution**.
- Tight wording: dissolution is mutual agreement to end; it is not a unilateral legal remedy.
- Section 123 BGB only becomes relevant if the dissolution agreement itself was induced by deceit or duress.

Quality: `green`

### Case 5: Fake Picasso

Prompt:

> A buyer purchases a painting because the seller intentionally lies that it is an original Picasso. Later the buyer discovers it is not. The buyer wants out. Which route is this: withdrawal, revocation, cancellation, rescission, or dissolution? Why?

User answer:

> its withdrawal but also incorporates the revocation due to the deceive in error of content?

Feedback:

- Correct route: **rescission**, not withdrawal.
- Anchor: Section 123 I Alt. 1 BGB, because the seller intentionally created or maintained an error.
- Correction: this is not an error of content. Error of content means the declaror misunderstands the meaning of their own words.

Quality: `yellow`

### Case 6: Rescission Ground Split

Prompt:

> 1. I type EUR 10,000 into an order form but meant to type EUR 1,000. 2. Seller tells me "this is an original Picasso," knowing it is fake. For each one: which rescission ground applies?

User answer:

> 1-error of content 2-error of declaration

Feedback:

- Correct split:
  - Typing EUR 10,000 instead of EUR 1,000 = **error of declaration**, Section 119 I Alt. 2 BGB.
  - Intentional lie about Picasso authorship = **deceit**, Section 123 I Alt. 1 BGB.
- Memory rule: wrong sign typed/spoken = error of declaration; wrong meaning of my own words = error of content; someone lied to me = deceit.

Quality: `red-yellow`

### Case 8: Custom-Made Sofa Online

Prompt:

> A consumer orders a custom-made sofa online. It arrives exactly as ordered, but the consumer changes their mind after delivery and wants to return it. Which route would you consider first, and what extra check might block it?

User answer:

> withdrawal, not meeting the deadlines for the withdrawal might block it

Feedback:

- Correct route: **withdrawal**.
- Main blocker in the fact pattern: custom-made goods may be excluded under Section 312g II BGB.
- The Section 355 BGB withdrawal period still matters, but the custom-made exclusion is the sharper trap.

Quality: `yellow-green`

### Case 9: Mutual Consulting Exit

Prompt:

> A consultant and a client agree to a 12-month service contract. After three months, both decide the project no longer makes sense and sign an agreement ending it immediately. Which route? Why is it not cancellation?

User answer:

> dissolution, both side okay with termination of agreement, not about a violence of obligation from one private authority

Feedback:

- Correct route: **dissolution**.
- Wording repair: say **breach/violation of duty**, not "violence of obligation."
- Wording repair: say **party** or **private autonomy**, not "authority," because private-law parties are legally equal.

Quality: `green` for routing, `yellow` for legal wording.

## Refined Mental Models

```text
Withdrawal = consumer cooling-off, no defect required.
Revocation = performance problem after a valid contract.
Cancellation = ongoing relationship ends for the future.
Dissolution = both parties agree to end.
Rescission = flawed declaration at formation.
```

```text
Wrong sign/number = error of declaration.
Wrong meaning of my own words = error of content.
Someone lied to me = deceit.
```
