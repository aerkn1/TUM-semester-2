## Short-Answer / Theory Questions

### Question 1

Define the consumer right of withdrawal and state the policy problem it solves.

**Model Answer:**
1. Withdrawal (Widerruf) is a statutory right allowing a consumer to exit certain contracts with a trader within a set period, without needing to show a defect or breach — a pure cooling-off right.
2. Statutory anchor: Sections 355, 312b, 312c BGB (355 for the declaration/effects mechanics; 312b for off-premises contracts; 312c for distance contracts).
3. Requirements/features: (1) personal scope — consumer (Section 13 BGB) contracting with a trader (Section 14 BGB); (2) material scope — a statutory withdrawal situation applies (most commonly distance or off-premises) with no exclusion under Section 312g II; (3) a clear declaration within the applicable time limit.
4. Example: a consumer buys a laptop from an online shop, receives it, and decides a few days later it does not meet their needs.
5. Consequence/trap: do not require any defect or wrongdoing by the trader — withdrawal exists precisely because the consumer faced structural disadvantage, information deficit, or surprise, not because anything went wrong with the goods.

### Question 2

Compare withdrawal and revocation.

**Model Answer:**
Withdrawal triggers when a consumer wants to exit a distance or off-premises contract with a trader, without any performance problem, anchored in Sections 355, 312b, 312c BGB, and leads to a consumer-protection restitution regime (Section 357) that is less strict than ordinary revocation restitution. Revocation triggers when a valid reciprocal contract exists but the debtor fails to perform, or performs badly, anchored in Sections 323, 324, 326 V BGB, and leads to full mutual restitution under Sections 346-349 BGB. Contrast example: a consumer who simply changes their mind about an online purchase needs withdrawal; a buyer who never receives the ordered goods at all after a Nachfrist expired needs revocation. Exam warning: do not reach for revocation just because a purchase was made online — check first whether anything actually went wrong with performance, or whether the consumer is merely exercising a cooling-off right.

### Question 3

State the four-step structure for extraordinary cancellation of a continuing obligation under Section 314 BGB.

**Model Answer:**
The requirements are:
1. A compelling reason — weighing both parties' interests and all circumstances, continuation until the agreed end or notice period cannot reasonably be expected of the cancelling party.
2. Expiry of a period for relief or a warning notice without result, unless dispensable (e.g. for gravity or special circumstances comparable to Section 323 II).
3. A declaration of cancellation within a reasonable period after gaining knowledge of the reason.
4. Effect: the continuing obligation ends immediately, ex nunc, without a notice period.

If these are met, the consequence is that the relationship ends for the future only — past performances are not undone, and damages can be combined under Section 314 IV BGB. The rule exists because continuing obligations (rent, employment, ongoing services) cannot usually be unwound retrospectively the way a one-time purchase can, so the law gives an immediate forward-looking exit instead of forcing retroactive restitution.

### Question 4

Compare cancellation and dissolution.

**Model Answer:**
Cancellation triggers when one party unilaterally ends a continuing obligation, either for a compelling reason (Section 314 BGB or a special provision) or, for indefinite-period relationships, by ordinary notice without needing a reason; it leads to termination ex nunc, with no restitution of past performances. Dissolution triggers when both parties agree to end a contract, resting on private autonomy (freedom to conclude contracts implies freedom to dissolve them by agreement); it leads to termination on whatever terms the parties negotiated (e.g. severance pay, paid leave, non-competition clauses). Contrast example: an employer who cannot easily cancel an employment relationship because of strong statutory protection may instead negotiate a dissolution agreement with the employee. Exam trap: dissolution is never unilateral — a single party's statement that they are "ending" the contract, without the other side's agreement, is not dissolution; it must be analyzed as cancellation or another exit route instead.

## Case / Legal-Opinion Questions

### Question 5 (Case)

**Facts:** A door-to-door salesperson rings the doorbell of consumer C's home, uninvited, and demonstrates a vacuum cleaner. C is persuaded on the spot and signs a purchase contract for EUR 800. The salesperson leaves without giving C any information about a right of withdrawal. Three days later, C has second thoughts and tells the trader by phone that she wants to return the vacuum cleaner and get her money back.

**Question:** Can C still effectively withdraw from the contract, and if so, within what time frame?

**Model Answer:**

I. Claim/issue sentence: C could be entitled to withdraw from the purchase contract with the trader under Sections 355, 312b BGB if the requirements of an off-premises withdrawal right are met.

II. Requirements:
- I. Purchase contract concluded (Section 433 BGB)
- II. Personal scope: consumer and trader
- III. Material scope: off-premises contract (Section 312b BGB)
- IV. No exclusion under Section 312g II BGB
- V. Declaration and timing (Section 355 BGB)

III. Application:
- I. A purchase contract for the vacuum cleaner was concluded when C signed on the spot.
- II. C acted for private, non-business purposes, so she is a consumer under Section 13 BGB; the salesperson's principal sells vacuum cleaners commercially, so it is a trader under Section 14 BGB.
- III. The contract was concluded in the simultaneous physical presence of both parties at a location other than the trader's business premises (C's home), following an unsolicited approach — this is the paradigm off-premises contract under Section 312b BGB.
- IV. Nothing in the facts suggests a custom-made or quickly perishable good; no exclusion applies.
- V. C's phone call three days later is a clear, unambiguous statement that she wants to return the goods — no specific form or the word "withdrawal" is required.

IV. Interim results: because no proper withdrawal instruction was given, the ordinary 14-day period under Section 355 II does not start running; under Section 356 III, the period is instead extended up to 12 months and 14 days from the point it otherwise would have started.

V. Final answer: C's declaration three days after signing falls well within the extended window, so C effectively withdraws from the contract; both parties must return what they received under the consumer withdrawal restitution regime (Section 357), and C is not required to give any justification.

### Question 6 (Case — integrates Week 05 + Week 03)

**Facts:** Consumer D calls trader T's telephone sales hotline. T's employee describes a designer lamp in detail and states a price of EUR 300. D immediately replies, "Yes, I'll take it, please send it to me." T ships the lamp the same week. T never sends D any information about a right of withdrawal. Ten months after receiving the lamp, D emails T saying the lamp does not suit her new interior and she wants to return it for a full refund.

**Question:** (a) Was a purchase contract validly concluded over the phone? (b) Can D still effectively withdraw ten months after delivery?

**Model Answer:**

**Part (a): Formation**

I. Claim/issue sentence: D and T concluded a valid purchase contract if there are two matching declarations of intent — offer and acceptance — and no validity obstacle applies (Section 433 BGB in conjunction with Sections 145-150 BGB).

II. Requirements:
- I. Offer: essentialia negotii present, clear intention to be bound (Section 145 BGB)
- II. Acceptance: matches the offer, timely under Section 147 I BGB
- III. No formation obstacle (Sections 125, 134, 138 BGB)

III. Application:
- I. T's employee named a specific lamp, described its features, and stated a fixed price of EUR 300 directly to D — this contains the essentialia negotii (parties, object, price) and, in a direct sales call naming a concrete price to a specific customer, shows a clear intention to be bound, so it is an offer, not a mere invitatio ad offerendum.
- II. A telephone call is treated as a declaration between parties who are "present" for timing purposes under Section 147 I BGB, so acceptance had to occur immediately during the call. D's "Yes, I'll take it, please send it" is an unconditional, matching acceptance given immediately.
- III. No statutory form is required for an ordinary consumer purchase of this kind, and nothing indicates a statutory prohibition or public-policy problem.

IV. Interim result: all formation requirements are met.

**Part (b): Withdrawal**

I. Claim/issue sentence: D could be entitled to withdraw from the purchase contract under Sections 355, 312c BGB if the requirements are met.

II. Requirements:
- I. Personal scope: consumer and trader
- II. Material scope: distance contract (Section 312c BGB)
- III. No exclusion under Section 312g II BGB
- IV. Declaration and timing (Sections 355, 356 BGB)

III. Application:
- I. D bought the lamp for private use, so she is a consumer (Section 13 BGB); T sells lamps commercially by phone, so T is a trader (Section 14 BGB).
- II. The contract was negotiated and concluded exclusively by phone, an organized means of distance communication, without simultaneous physical presence — a distance contract under Section 312c BGB.
- III. A standard designer lamp sold from stock is not custom-made or perishable; no exclusion applies.
- IV. T never gave D the required withdrawal instructions. Under Section 356 III BGB, the ordinary 14-day period (which would otherwise start on receipt of the goods, Section 356 II No. 1) does not begin, and the withdrawal right is instead capped at 12 months and 14 days from the point it would have started.

IV. Interim result: D's email ten months after delivery falls inside the extended 12-months-plus-14-days window.

V. Final answer: the purchase contract was validly concluded over the phone through immediate offer and acceptance under Sections 145 and 147 I BGB, and because T failed to instruct D on her withdrawal right, D's withdrawal ten months later is still effective under Section 356 III BGB — D may return the lamp and reclaim the EUR 300.

### Question 7 (Case)

**Facts:** Consultant K has a two-year advisory service contract with company U. Six months into the relationship, U's new CEO publicly accuses K of fraud in a press interview, with no factual basis, seriously damaging K's professional reputation, and U also stops paying K's last two invoices without explanation. K wants to end the contract immediately rather than wait out the remaining eighteen months.

**Question:** Can K extraordinarily cancel the service contract with immediate effect?

**Model Answer:**

I. Claim/issue sentence: K could be entitled to extraordinarily cancel the continuing service obligation under Section 314 BGB if the requirements are met.

II. Requirements:
- I. Compelling reason
- II. Expiry of a relief/warning period without result, unless dispensable
- III. Declaration of cancellation within a reasonable period
- IV. Effect: ex nunc

III. Application:
- I. Questionable is whether a compelling reason exists. This is supported by the fact that the CEO's public, unfounded fraud accusation is a serious breach of the duty of consideration owed within the relationship (Section 241 II BGB), and combined with the unexplained non-payment, it destroys the mutual trust an ongoing advisory relationship depends on. Against this, one could argue a single public statement might be an isolated lapse rather than a fundamental breakdown; however, a public reputational attack of this severity, paired with withheld payment, makes continuing the relationship for another eighteen months unreasonable for K when weighing both parties' interests.
- II. A relief or warning period would ordinarily be required first. However, gravity comparable to a serious and final refusal (analogous to Section 323 II BGB) can make this dispensable — publicly and falsely accusing a contract partner of fraud is not the kind of breach a warning notice could meaningfully cure, since the reputational harm and trust breakdown are already done.
- III. K should declare the cancellation without unreasonable delay after learning of the interview and the missed payments to stay within a reasonable period.

IV. Interim results: compelling reason met; warning period dispensable given the gravity; timely declaration assumed on the facts given.

V. Final answer: K can extraordinarily cancel the service contract with immediate effect under Section 314 BGB. The contract remains valid and owed for the first six months already performed — there is no retrospective restitution — and K may combine the cancellation with a damages claim under Section 314 IV BGB for the unpaid invoices and any recoverable reputational harm.

### Question 8 (Case)

**Facts:** Consumer M orders a custom-tailored suit from an online tailoring shop, submitting his exact body measurements and selecting a specific fabric and color combination made only for him. Ten days after receiving the finished suit, M decides he does not like how the chosen color looks in person and wants to withdraw from the contract for a full refund, even though the suit was made exactly to his specifications.

**Question:** Can M withdraw from the purchase contract?

**Model Answer:**

I. Claim/issue sentence: M could be entitled to withdraw from the purchase contract with the online tailor under Sections 355, 312c BGB if the requirements are met.

II. Requirements:
- I. Personal scope: consumer and trader
- II. Material scope: distance contract (Section 312c BGB)
- III. No exclusion under Section 312g II BGB
- IV. Declaration and timing (Section 355 BGB)

III. Application:
- I. M ordered the suit for private use, so he is a consumer (Section 13 BGB); the online tailoring shop sells commercially, so it is a trader (Section 14 BGB).
- II. The order was placed and concluded exclusively online, an organized distance-sales scheme — a distance contract under Section 312c BGB.
- III. Questionable is whether an exclusion applies. This is supported by Section 312g II BGB, which excludes the withdrawal right for goods manufactured to consumer specifications or clearly personalized to the consumer. A suit made to M's exact measurements and his chosen fabric/color combination is manufactured specifically for him and cannot be resold to another customer without substantial loss — it fits squarely within this exclusion.
- IV. Because the material-scope requirement already fails at the exclusion step, the declaration/timing requirement does not need to be reached.

IV. Interim result: the material-scope requirement is not met — the custom-made exclusion applies.

V. Final answer: M cannot withdraw from the purchase contract. The customization exclusion under Section 312g II BGB blocks the statutory withdrawal right despite the contract otherwise qualifying as a distance contract, so M remains bound to the suit purchase regardless of his change of mind about the color.

## Quick Answer Route Summary

| Question # | Primary Route/Section | One-Line Takeaway |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Sections 355, 312b, 312c BGB | Withdrawal needs no defect — it is a pure cooling-off right. |
| 2 | Withdrawal vs. Sections 323/324/326 V, 346-349 BGB | Withdrawal = no performance problem; revocation = performance failure in a valid contract. |
| 3 | Section 314 BGB | Compelling reason -> relief/warning (unless dispensable) -> timely declaration -> ex nunc effect. |
| 4 | Cancellation vs. dissolution | Cancellation is unilateral with a reason/notice; dissolution always needs agreement. |
| 5 | Sections 355, 312b, 356 III BGB | Missing withdrawal instructions extend the clock up to 12 months + 14 days. |
| 6 | Sections 145, 147 I BGB (formation) + Sections 355, 312c, 356 III BGB (withdrawal) | Confirm phone-call formation first, then run the withdrawal route on top of it. |
| 7 | Section 314 BGB | Severe trust-destroying breach can make the warning/relief period dispensable. |
| 8 | Section 312g II BGB | Custom-made-to-order goods are excluded from withdrawal even inside a valid distance contract. |
