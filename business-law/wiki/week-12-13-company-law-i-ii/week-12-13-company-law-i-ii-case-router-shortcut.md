## One-Sentence Core

Company Law asks: who is the legal actor, who can act for it, who bears the debt, who controls the inside, and whether the chosen legal form fits the business risk.

```text
venture/risk -> legal form -> representation -> liability -> internal body -> business consequence
```

If a case has "GmbH", "UG", "AG", "oHG", "KG", "GmbH & Co. KG", "shareholder", "director", "partner", "Prokurist", "capital", "register", or "limited liability", run Company Law before jumping to ordinary contract/property/warranty conclusions.

## 30-Second Trigger Router

| Fact pattern signal | Immediate route | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Founders ask which business form to choose | Legal-form choice | Match risk, capital, financing, governance, tax/disclosure, reputation, and transferability. |
| Two people start a modest joint activity | GbR vs oHG | A small non-commercial joint venture usually starts as GbR; oHG needs commercial-business status. |
| Commercial partnership with all active partners | oHG | All partners are personally liable; do not invent a liability cap. |
| Active manager plus passive investor | KG | Komplementaer manages and is unlimitedly liable; Kommanditist is contribution-limited. |
| KG with a GmbH as general partner | GmbH & Co. KG | The unlimited-liability role exists, but practical exposure shifts to the GmbH's assets. |
| Low capital but need liability shield | UG | Mini-GmbH route, but cash-only and 25% annual-surplus reserve duty. |
| SME with risk and enough capital | GmbH | Separate legal person, shareholder liability shield, EUR 25,000 capital, director representation. |
| Large investor/capital-market setup | AG | Rigid dualistic governance, EUR 50,000 capital stock, easier share transfer/capital raising. |
| GmbH director signs contract above internal limit | GmbH organ representation | Section 35 GmbHG gives representation; Section 37 GmbHG usually keeps internal limits external-ineffective. |
| Director contracts with self or own side business | Agency/self-dealing plus director duties | Section 181 BGB can block the transaction; Section 43 GmbHG can create director liability. |
| Shareholder tries to run daily AG business | AG governance | General assembly does not instruct the management board in daily management. |
| Company sells goods/assets | Company plus property/warranty | The company owns/sells assets through organs or agents; shareholders do not own company assets. |

## Master Sequence For Mixed Cases

Use this order when the case combines several weeks:

```text
1. Is the actor a person, partnership, or corporation?
2. If company/partnership: identify exact legal form.
3. Ask who can represent that legal form externally.
4. Check internal limits separately from external power.
5. Check special authority limits: self-dealing, Prokura, register reliance.
6. Decide who is bound by the contract.
7. Decide whose assets can be reached for liability.
8. Only then run property transfer, warranty, SBT, damages, or rescission.
9. End with business consequence: suitable form, creditor target, or valid/invalid transaction.
```

Shortcut: in exam writing, never say "the shareholder bought/sold" if the facts say the GmbH did. Say "the GmbH acted through its managing director."

## Legal Form Picker

| Need in the facts | Best first candidate | Reject or warn about |
|---|---|---|
| Tiny/simple joint purpose, low formality, no clear commercial scale | GbR | Warn: no built-in private-asset shield. |
| Commercial operation by partners willing to accept personal exposure | oHG | Bad fit if founders want liability limitation. |
| One active entrepreneur plus passive capital provider | KG | Komplementaer remains unlimitedly liable. |
| Partnership flexibility but no natural-person unlimited exposure | GmbH & Co. KG | More complex structure; the general partner is still liable, but it is a GmbH. |
| Limited liability, SME credibility, investor plans, enough capital | GmbH | Costs, formalities, disclosure/accounting, EUR 25,000 capital. |
| Limited liability with very little startup capital | UG | Cash-only, lower reputation, bank guarantees may still be demanded, reserve duty. |
| Broad capital raising, transferable shares, larger business | AG | Rigid bodies, higher capital, less direct shareholder control. |

Exam rule: a legal-form recommendation must include at least one rejected alternative. A pure "choose GmbH because limited liability" answer is too thin.

## All High-Yield Combinations

| Combination | Route | Exam conclusion pattern |
|---|---|---|
| GbR vs oHG | common purpose first, then commercial scale | Joint activity alone is not enough for oHG; modest private-customer activity remains GbR. |
| oHG debt + partner did not sign | oHG partner liability | Partner can be personally liable even if another partner created the business debt. |
| KG debt + limited partner paid contribution | KG liability split | Komplementaer unlimited; Kommanditist generally not beyond agreed and paid contribution. |
| GmbH & Co. KG debt | hybrid liability | Creditor reaches KG assets and Komplementaer GmbH assets, not natural-person shareholders by default. |
| GmbH named in facts | Section 13 GmbHG route | GmbH is independent legal person; company liable with company assets. |
| GmbH shareholder sued personally | liability shield route | No personal shareholder liability in ordinary case once contribution duties are fulfilled. |
| GmbH not registered yet | formation route | No full GmbH before commercial-register registration; registration is constitutive. |
| Overvalued in-kind contribution | capital protection | Paper capital is not genuine capital if the asset value is inflated; creditor-protection purpose fails. |
| GmbH director signs contract | Section 35 GmbHG | Director can represent the GmbH externally. |
| GmbH director exceeds articles/internal approval limit | Section 37 GmbHG | Internal limit usually does not defeat external representation; possible internal director liability. |
| GmbH director signs with own private business | Section 181 BGB | Self-dealing can block/provisionally invalidate unless exception or ratification applies. |
| GmbH director secretly competes | Section 43 GmbHG | Duty of loyalty breach; director may owe damages to the company. |
| GmbH insolvent and director delays filing | Section 43 GmbHG plus Section 15a InsO | Insolvency filing duty breach can trigger personal director liability. |
| UG earns profit | Section 5a GmbHG | Reserve at least 25% of annual surplus until GmbH capital threshold is reached. |
| GmbH as merchant | Section 6 HGB plus GmbH status | Merchant by legal form; skip Section 1 HGB scale test. |
| GmbH and Prokura | Company Law plus Trade Law | Director/shareholder body grants power; HGB controls external Prokura scope and internal-limit effects. |
| AG shareholder wants to instruct management board | AG dualistic system | General assembly has fundamental competences; management board manages independently. |
| Supervisory board tells management board to choose supplier | AG governance | Supervisory board supervises/appoints, but does not run daily business. |
| GmbH sells van/machine | Company Law plus Week 10 property | GmbH forms disposition agreement through director; delivery can be carried out by employee. |
| Shareholder claims company asset is "his" | entity separation | Shareholder owns shares, not the GmbH's van, cash, inventory, or claims. |
| Public university buys from GmbH | Intro public/private law | If the university buys as ordinary market participant, dispute is private law. |

## Representation Shortcut

```text
ordinary person -> ordinary BGB agency if someone acts for them
GmbH -> managing director represents externally
UG -> same logic as GmbH, because UG is a GmbH variant
AG -> management board represents/manages
oHG/KG -> partner-based HGB representation, then special rules if Prokura appears
GmbH & Co. KG -> GmbH general partner acts through its managing director
```

Internal/external split:

```text
Can the representative bind the entity externally?
Can the representative do this internally without breaching duties/instructions?
```

These are different questions. In Company Law, many exam traps hide in that split.

## Liability Shortcut

| Legal form | First asset pool | Private-asset exposure |
|---|---|---|
| GbR | Partnership assets | Partners usually personally exposed. |
| oHG | Partnership assets | All partners personally, unlimitedly exposed. |
| KG | KG assets | Komplementaer unlimited; Kommanditist capped if contribution paid. |
| GmbH & Co. KG | KG assets, then Komplementaer GmbH assets | Natural-person shareholders not personally exposed by default. |
| GmbH | GmbH assets | Shareholders generally shielded after contribution duties. |
| UG | UG assets | Same liability idea as GmbH, but lower capital/reputation. |
| AG | AG assets | Shareholders generally shielded; management/supervisory duties are separate. |

Trap correction: "limited liability" does not mean nobody pays. It means the company still pays; owners' private assets are normally shielded.

## Governance Shortcut

### GmbH

```text
shareholders own/control basics
shareholder meeting appoints/dismisses directors and decides profit use
managing directors manage and represent externally
director breach -> possible liability to the GmbH
```

GmbH is flexible: shareholder control over directors is much stronger than in an AG.

### AG

```text
general assembly -> elects/decides fundamentals
supervisory board -> appoints, dismisses, supervises management board
management board -> manages and represents independently
```

AG is rigid: shareholders do not directly run daily business through instructions to the management board.

## Fast Case-Reading Algorithm

### A. Legal-Form Recommendation Case

```text
1. Identify activity size and risk.
2. Identify capital available.
3. Identify founder relationship and control needs.
4. Identify financing/investor/reputation needs.
5. Pick candidate form.
6. Reject at least one alternative.
7. State liability, governance, formation, and business consequence.
```

Template:

```text
The suitable form is likely [FORM], because [risk/capital/control facts]. [FORM] gives [liability/governance benefit], but has [cost/formality/downside]. [ALTERNATIVE] is weaker because [reason]. Therefore [FORM] best matches the business plan.
```

### B. Company Contract Case

```text
1. Who is named as contracting party?
2. Which organ/agent acted?
3. Did that person have external representation power?
4. Was there an internal restriction?
5. Was there a special external defect, especially Section 181 BGB?
6. Result: entity bound or not bound.
7. Separate internal liability/duty breach.
```

### C. Creditor Liability Case

```text
1. Identify debtor entity.
2. Identify legal form.
3. Identify first asset pool.
4. Ask whether partner/shareholder/director personal liability exists.
5. Apply form-specific split.
6. End with exact asset target.
```

### D. Governance Case

```text
1. Identify GmbH or AG.
2. Identify body: shareholder meeting, director, general assembly, supervisory board, management board.
3. Ask whether that body has the competence.
4. If not, say which body does.
5. Separate invalid instruction from possible internal consequences.
```

## Answer Skeletons

### Legal Opinion Skeleton

```text
I. Issue sentence:
[Entity/person] could be bound/liable/entitled if [legal form + representation/liability rule] applies.

II. Requirements:
1. Exact legal form.
2. Proper representative or competent body.
3. Liability or validity consequence.
4. Any exception or special block.

III. Application:
The facts show [fact]. This points to [rule]. Against this, [counterfact] might matter. However, [why rule wins]. Therefore [requirement met/not met].

IV. Interim result:
[Entity] is/is not bound/liable; [person] is/is not personally exposed.

V. Final answer:
The creditor/buyer/shareholder can [exact consequence] against [exact legal actor or asset pool].
```

### Theory Skeleton

```text
Definition -> statutory anchor -> function -> example -> trap.
```

Example:

```text
A GmbH is an independent legal person under GmbHG rules. Its function is to let the company act and be liable with company assets while shareholders are generally shielded after contribution duties. Example: the supplier sues the GmbH, not the shareholder personally. Trap: limited liability is not no liability.
```

## 12-Minute Drill

Do this closed-book. Write only route words and final consequence.

1. Two friends run a modest private gardening service, no register, low revenue. GbR or oHG?
2. A registered oHG buys EUR 40,000 inventory through partner A. Can creditor pursue partner B?
3. KG has K as Komplementaer and L as Kommanditist; L paid the agreed contribution. Can creditor pursue L personally for more?
4. GmbH director signs a purchase above the EUR 50,000 internal approval limit. Is GmbH externally bound?
5. Same director signs a consulting contract with their own private business. Which extra rule?
6. UG earns EUR 40,000 annual surplus. Can owner distribute all of it?
7. Founder contributes a machine valued on paper at EUR 20,000 but actually worth EUR 9,000. What is the capital problem?
8. AG supervisory board orders management board to select Supplier X. Must management board obey?
9. GmbH sells a van. Who forms the disposition agreement for Week 10 ownership transfer?
10. GmbH & Co. KG cannot pay. Which assets can creditor reach first and next?
11. Public university buys equipment from GmbH like an ordinary customer. Public or private law?
12. GmbH is tiny and newly formed. Is it a merchant only if Section 1 HGB commercial scale is met?

Answers:

1. GbR unless commercial scale pushes to oHG.
2. Yes, oHG partners are personally liable for partnership debts.
3. Generally no beyond the agreed and paid contribution; pursue KG and Komplementaer.
4. Usually yes externally under Sections 35/37 GmbHG; internal breach remains possible.
5. Section 181 BGB self-dealing, plus possible Section 43 GmbHG duty breach.
6. No; reserve at least 25% until GmbH threshold is reached.
7. In-kind contribution overvaluation means capital is not genuinely satisfied.
8. No; AG management board manages independently, supervised but not instructed day-to-day.
9. The GmbH acts through its managing director; handover can be done by an employee.
10. KG assets, then the Komplementaer GmbH's assets; not natural-person shareholders by default.
11. Private law if the university acts as market participant.
12. No; GmbH is merchant by legal form under HGB/GmbHG logic.

## Retention Hook

```text
Company Law = legal skin of the business.

Skin decides:
- who speaks for the business
- whose pocket pays
- who controls management
- what later BGB/HGB/property/remedy rules attach to
```

When lost, ask: "Am I analyzing a human, the entity, an organ, or an asset pool?"
