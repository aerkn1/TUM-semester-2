## Short-Answer / Theory Questions

### Question 1

What makes a person or entity a merchant (Kaufmann) under the HGB, and why does this classification matter for a business-law exam case?

**Model Answer:**
1. A merchant is a person or entity carrying on a commercial business (Section 1 I HGB), or treated as a merchant through voluntary registration (Section 2 HGB) or automatically by legal form (Section 6 I HGB, Section 13 III GmbHG).
2. Statutory anchor: Sections 1, 2, 6 HGB; Section 13 III GmbHG.
3. Requirements/features: (1) Section 1 I HGB — actually carrying on a commercially organized business, presumed unless the type/scope makes commercial organization unnecessary; (2) Section 2 HGB — a smaller business becomes a merchant only by opting into registration; (3) Section 6 I HGB / Section 13 III GmbHG — certain legal forms (GmbH, AG) are merchants automatically, regardless of actual business scale.
4. Example: a one-person online retailer running an organized accounting/logistics operation is a merchant under Section 1 I HGB without ever registering; a small part-time craft seller only becomes one by registering under Section 2 HGB; a freshly founded GmbH is a merchant from the start under Section 6 I HGB even before it makes its first sale.
5. Consequence/trap: merchant status is the gateway to the entire HGB layer — Prokura, Section 15 publicity, the Section 362/confirmation-letter silence exceptions, and the Section 377 notice duty all presuppose it. "The actor is a company" is not itself an answer — name the specific gateway (Section 1, 2, or 6 HGB).

### Question 2

Compare Prokura and Handlungsvollmacht.

**Model Answer:**
Prokura triggers when the merchant (or an organ authorized to represent the merchant) expressly grants it, anchored in Sections 48-53 HGB, and leads to a very broad, legally fixed scope covering nearly all business acts (Section 49 I HGB), with internal restrictions generally ineffective against third parties (Section 50 HGB).

Handlungsvollmacht triggers when a merchant puts someone in charge of a kind of business or specific acts without granting full Prokura, anchored in Section 54 HGB, and leads to authority scoped to what that kind of business or transaction usually entails — excluding loan-taking and court representation unless separately authorized (Section 54 II HGB) — where individually known or should-have-known limits can bind externally.

Contrast example: a Prokurist can validly sign a EUR 50,000 supply contract even against an unregistered internal EUR 20,000 cap, because a third party who doesn't know of the cap isn't bound by it; an employee with Handlungsvollmacht subject to a supplier-known EUR 20,000 cap cannot.

Exam warning: do not treat every "authorized signatory" as a Prokurist — only an actual express grant under Section 48 HGB creates Prokura; anything narrower defaults to the Handlungsvollmacht analysis instead.

### Question 3

State the effect of Section 15 I, II, and III HGB in one sentence each.

**Model Answer:**
The requirements/effects are:
1. Section 15 I HGB: if a fact requiring registration is true but was never registered, a good-faith third party may rely on the register's silence, and the merchant generally cannot assert the unregistered true fact against them.
2. Section 15 II HGB: if a fact requiring registration is true, registered, and published, it can be asserted against third parties regardless of their actual knowledge.
3. Section 15 III HGB: if the register wrongly shows a fact that is not actually true, a good-faith third party may rely on that wrong published content.

If these are met, the consequence is that the register's public face — rather than the underlying truth — controls what a good-faith third party can rely on.

This exists because the commercial register is meant to be the reliable single source of truth for legal traffic, sparing third parties from independently investigating every counterpart's true legal status.

### Question 4

List the requirements for Section 377 HGB to exclude a buyer's warranty rights.

**Model Answer:**
The requirements are:
1. A mutual commercial purchase under Section 343 HGB (both parties act as merchants within their businesses).
2. Delivery of the goods to the buyer or a third party the buyer designated.
3. A material defect under Section 434 BGB present in the delivered goods.
4. The buyer's failure to inspect within due course and notify the defect without undue delay — immediately upon discovery for defects not recognizable on ordinary inspection.
5. No fraudulent concealment of the defect by the seller.

If these are met, the consequence is that the goods are deemed approved and the buyer's warranty rights under Section 437 BGB are excluded for that defect.

This exists to give commercial sellers fast certainty about whether a transaction is closed, reflecting the higher professionalism expected of merchants compared to ordinary BGB buyers.

## Case / Legal-Opinion Questions

### Question 5 (Case)

**Facts:** König Elektro GmbH grants Prokura to its purchasing manager, Frau Berger, registered in the commercial register. Internally, the managing director instructs Berger never to sign contracts above EUR 30,000 without his prior sign-off. Needing components urgently, Berger signs a EUR 45,000 supply contract with Kupfer AG on König Elektro GmbH's behalf, signing "ppa. Berger." Kupfer AG's sales representative has never heard of any internal limit and had no reason to suspect one. König Elektro GmbH later refuses to pay, citing the internal EUR 30,000 cap.

**Question:** Does Kupfer AG have a claim against König Elektro GmbH for payment of EUR 45,000 under Section 433 II BGB?

**Model Answer:**

I. Claim/issue sentence: Kupfer AG could have a claim against König Elektro GmbH for payment of EUR 45,000 under Section 433 II BGB if a valid purchase contract was concluded and binds the GmbH.

II. Requirements:
- I. Merchant status of König Elektro GmbH
- II. Contract conclusion between Berger (for the GmbH) and Kupfer AG
- III. Effective representation of König Elektro GmbH by Berger
- IV. No effective internal limitation defeating that representation
- V. Legal consequence

III. Application:
- I. König Elektro GmbH is a merchant by legal form under Section 6 I HGB / Section 13 III GmbHG, regardless of its actual business scale — the merchant gateway is met without the Section 1 scale test.
- II. Berger and Kupfer AG's representative exchanged a matching offer and acceptance for the EUR 45,000 supply contract.
- III. Questionable is whether Berger's declaration binds König Elektro GmbH. This is supported by the fact that Berger holds registered Prokura under Section 48 HGB, which under Section 49 I HGB covers nearly all business acts a commercial enterprise entails, including ordinary supply purchases — she acted with her own declaration and signed "ppa.," satisfying the Section 164 I BGB publicity element.
- IV. Against binding the GmbH, one could argue the internal EUR 30,000 cap should control. However, under Section 50 I HGB, internal restrictions on Prokura are ineffective against third parties unless the third party knew of the limitation or its breach was obvious. Kupfer AG's representative had no such knowledge and nothing in the facts made a limit obvious. Consequently, the internal limit does not defeat Berger's external authority.

IV. Interim results: merchant status met; contract concluded; representation effective despite the internal cap.

V. Final answer: As a result, Kupfer AG can claim payment of EUR 45,000 from König Elektro GmbH under Section 433 II BGB; the internal EUR 30,000 limit only gives the GmbH an internal claim against Berger, not a defense against Kupfer AG.

### Question 6 (Case)

**Facts:** Herr Novak revokes the Prokura of his employee, Herr Adler, after a dispute, but forgets to have the revocation entered in the commercial register. Two months later, Adler — still holding the company stamp — signs a EUR 12,000 purchase contract "ppa." with Fenster GmbH, whose purchasing agent checked the commercial register beforehand and saw Adler listed as Prokurist with no revocation noted. Novak now refuses to honor the contract, telling Fenster GmbH that Adler's Prokura had already been revoked.

**Question:** Can Fenster GmbH hold Novak's business bound to the EUR 12,000 contract?

**Model Answer:**

I. Claim/issue sentence: Fenster GmbH could have a claim against Novak's business for performance of the EUR 12,000 contract under Section 433 I BGB if the contract concluded through Adler binds the business despite the (unregistered) revocation of his Prokura.

II. Requirements:
- I. Novak's business is a merchant and the revocation is a registrable fact
- II. The revocation is true
- III. The fact was not registered
- IV. Fenster GmbH acted in good faith
- V. Legal consequence under Section 15 I HGB

III. Application:
- I. Novak's business is a merchant, so the grant and revocation of Prokura are facts Section 53 HGB requires to be registered.
- II. The revocation is true — Adler's Prokura had in fact ended.
- III. Questionable is whether the revocation can be asserted against Fenster GmbH. Against this, one could argue a principal should always be free to end an agent's authority effective immediately. However, Novak never had the revocation entered in the register, so the true fact remained unregistered.
- IV. This is supported by Fenster GmbH's agent having checked the register beforehand and found nothing indicating revocation, with no other reason to doubt Adler's status — satisfying good faith.

IV. Interim results: unregistered true fact confirmed; good faith confirmed; Section 15 I HGB protection applies.

V. Final answer: As a result, under Section 15 I HGB, Novak cannot assert the true but unregistered revocation against a good-faith third party who relied on the register's silence — Fenster GmbH can hold Novak's business bound to the EUR 12,000 contract as if Adler's Prokura were still valid.

### Question 7 (Case)

**Facts:** Merchant seller Dynamo GmbH delivers 50 industrial pumps to merchant buyer Kraft OHG on 3 March. The pumps have a visible manufacturing defect (cracked housings) that would be apparent on a routine visual check. Kraft OHG's warehouse staff sign for delivery but do not inspect the shipment until 20 March, when they notice the cracks and notify Dynamo GmbH the same day. Kraft OHG demands replacement under Section 439 BGB. Dynamo GmbH refuses, citing Section 377 HGB.

**Question:** Can Kraft OHG demand replacement of the pumps under Section 439 BGB?

**Model Answer:**

I. Claim/issue sentence: Kraft OHG could have a claim against Dynamo GmbH for replacement of the pumps under Sections 437 No. 1, 439 BGB if the pumps are defective and Kraft OHG's warranty rights were not excluded under Section 377 HGB.

II. Requirements:
- I. Mutual commercial purchase (Section 343 HGB)
- II. Delivery
- III. Material defect (Section 434 BGB)
- IV. Timely inspection and notice under Section 377 HGB
- V. Legal consequence

III. Application:
- I. Both Dynamo GmbH and Kraft OHG are merchants acting within their businesses, so this is a mutual commercial purchase under Section 343 HGB.
- II. Delivery of the 50 pumps occurred on 3 March.
- III. The cracked housings are a material defect under Section 434 BGB — the pumps lack the quality customary for defect-free industrial pumps.
- IV. Questionable is whether Kraft OHG met its Section 377 HGB duty. This is supported by notice being given the same day the defect was actually noticed. However, the defect was recognizable on a routine inspection that should have occurred shortly after the 3 March delivery, not 17 days later. Against Kraft OHG, one could argue warehouse workload excuses the delay, but Section 377 HGB expects inspection without undue delay after delivery for defects recognizable on ordinary inspection, and a 17-day gap does not meet that standard here.

IV. Interim results: mutual commercial purchase confirmed; defect confirmed; Section 377 HGB duty breached.

V. Final answer: As a result, the pumps are deemed approved under Section 377 HGB, Kraft OHG's warranty rights are excluded, and Kraft OHG cannot demand replacement under Section 439 BGB despite the pumps being objectively defective.

### Question 8 (Case — integrates Week 11 + Week 12-13)

**Facts:** Rapid Logistik GmbH & Co. KG needs a new forklift. The KG's general partner (Komplementär) is Rapid Verwaltungs GmbH, whose sole managing director, Herr Thies, has granted Prokura to the KG's logistics manager, Frau Voss, registered in the commercial register. Voss negotiates and signs a EUR 28,000 forklift purchase contract with Gabelstapler AG, signing "ppa. Voss for Rapid Logistik GmbH & Co. KG." Gabelstapler AG later learns that Thies never personally countersigned this specific contract and questions whether the KG is bound at all, given the layered GmbH & Co. KG structure.

**Question:** Is Rapid Logistik GmbH & Co. KG bound to pay EUR 28,000 to Gabelstapler AG under Section 433 II BGB?

**Model Answer:**

I. Claim/issue sentence: Gabelstapler AG could have a claim against Rapid Logistik GmbH & Co. KG for payment of EUR 28,000 under Section 433 II BGB if a valid purchase contract was concluded and binds the KG through Voss's Prokura.

II. Requirements:
- I. Merchant status of the KG
- II. Contract conclusion
- III. Effective grant of Prokura within the layered GmbH & Co. KG structure
- IV. Effective representation by Voss under that Prokura
- V. Legal consequence

III. Application:
- I. A KG carrying on a commercial business is a merchant under Sections 1, 6 HGB in conjunction with Sections 105 ff., 161 HGB. The GmbH & Co. KG structure does not change this — the KG itself is the merchant and contracting party, with Rapid Verwaltungs GmbH acting only as its general partner.
- II. Voss and Gabelstapler AG's representative reached agreement on the EUR 28,000 forklift purchase — a valid offer and acceptance exist.
- III. Questionable is whether Voss's Prokura was validly granted. This is supported by the fact that Prokura for a KG must be granted by whoever is entitled to represent the KG — here, the general partner Rapid Verwaltungs GmbH, itself represented by its managing director Thies under Section 35 GmbHG. Thies, acting for the GmbH in its capacity as general partner, validly granted Voss Prokura, registered under Section 53 HGB. Against this, one could argue Thies's absence from the specific forklift contract matters. However, that misreads the structure: Thies's involvement was only required at the earlier stage of granting Prokura to Voss, not for each individual transaction she later concludes under it.
- IV. Once validly granted, Voss's Prokura carries the broad statutory scope of Section 49 I HGB, covering an ordinary equipment purchase for logistics operations; she signed "ppa." for the KG, satisfying the Section 164 I BGB publicity element, and nothing in the facts suggests any known or obvious internal limitation.

IV. Interim results: KG is a merchant; contract concluded; Prokura validly granted through the general-partner GmbH's managing director; representation by Voss effective.

V. Final answer: As a result, Rapid Logistik GmbH & Co. KG is bound to pay EUR 28,000 to Gabelstapler AG under Section 433 II BGB. The layered GmbH & Co. KG structure only changes who is competent to grant Prokura (the general partner's managing director) — it does not require that person's personal involvement in every transaction the Prokurist later undertakes.

## Quick Answer Route Summary

| Question # | Primary Route/Section | One-line takeaway |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Sections 1, 2, 6 HGB | Merchant status has three separate gateways — scale, opt-in registration, or legal form. |
| 2 | Sections 48-54 HGB | Prokura is broad and hard to restrict; Handlungsvollmacht is narrower and easier to restrict. |
| 3 | Section 15 I-III HGB | The register's public face controls good-faith reliance, whether silent, accurate, or wrong. |
| 4 | Section 377 HGB | Five cumulative requirements before warranty rights are excluded for late notice. |
| 5 | Sections 48-50 HGB | Internal Prokura limits don't bind an unaware third party. |
| 6 | Section 15 I HGB | Unregistered true revocation cannot be asserted against a good-faith third party relying on register silence. |
| 7 | Section 377 HGB | A 17-day gap on an obvious defect is too slow — warranty rights are excluded. |
| 8 | Sections 48-50 HGB + Section 35 GmbHG | Prokura in a GmbH & Co. KG is granted through the general-partner GmbH's managing director, not re-confirmed per transaction. |
