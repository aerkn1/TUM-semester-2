## Clarification Questions

### Prompt 1

User question:

> For warranty rights-i, I need clarification: what is actually the cure and the disproportion cases? give with real examples

Professor clarification:

`Cure` is the buyer's primary remedy under Sections 437 No. 1 and 439 BGB. It means the seller gets a second chance to make the purchase performance defect-free.

The buyer can generally choose:

- repair / rectification: remove the defect from the delivered thing;
- replacement delivery / re-delivery: deliver a defect-free substitute thing.

Disproportion does not mean seller inconvenience. It means the buyer's selected cure type is possible only at disproportionate expense, considering the value of the thing when defect-free, the significance of the defect, and whether the alternative cure type can be used without substantial disadvantage to the buyer.

Examples:

- Small headphone button defect: one-day repair costs EUR 20 and is flawless; replacement costs EUR 100 plus handling. Replacement can likely be refused as disproportionate; repair remains available.
- New phone overheats unpredictably: repair would require major internal work and weeks of waiting; replacement is quick. Replacement is less likely to be disproportionate because the defect is serious and repair disadvantages the buyer.
- Wrong material case: a table sold as oak is actually veneer. Repair cannot turn veneer into oak; replacement is the meaningful cure if available.

Exam sentence:

```text
The buyer may choose repair or replacement under Section 439 I BGB, but the seller may refuse the chosen type under Section 439 IV BGB if it is possible only at disproportionate expense.
```

### Prompt 2

User question:

> Then if no agreement between customer and seller over cure, it goes through revocation?

Professor clarification:

No. Mere disagreement about cure does not automatically open revocation.

Correct route:

```text
defect
-> buyer chooses cure
-> seller must cure unless there is a valid refusal reason
-> if one cure type is disproportionate, use the other cure type if reasonable
-> revocation/reduction/damages only if cure as such fails, is refused, is impossible, unreasonable, or the period expires
```

Key correction: disagreement over the preferred cure type is not the same as failed cure.

Example:

- Buyer wants replacement headphones.
- Seller offers quick, flawless repair.
- Replacement is much more expensive and repair creates no substantial disadvantage.
- Result: no immediate revocation; buyer is limited to repair.

Opposite example:

- Seller refuses both repair and replacement.
- No meaningful second chance remains.
- Result: Section 440 BGB logic can open revocation, reduction, or damages without another cure period.

### Prompt 3

User question:

> Is this revocation different than the one in we discussed in contract law?

Professor clarification:

Warranty revocation is not a completely different remedy. It is the purchase-defect version of the general revocation concept.

Comparison:

| General Contract-Law Revocation | Warranty-Law Revocation |
|---|---|
| General breach route, mainly Section 323 BGB. | Defective purchase route through Section 437 No. 2 BGB. |
| Trigger: non-performance or improper performance of a due obligation. | Trigger: defect at transfer of risk. |
| Usually requires a reasonable additional period. | Usually requires cure priority under Section 439 BGB. |
| Example: buyer does not pay after deadline. | Example: seller delivers defective goods and cure fails. |

Exam sentence:

```text
Warranty revocation is a special application of revocation inside the purchase-defect system. It assumes a valid contract and unwinds it because defective performance was not cured.
```

Do not confuse this with rescission. Rescission attacks the declaration of intent because of mistake, deceit, or duress. Warranty revocation assumes the contract is valid.

### Prompt 4

User question:

> So the warranty version adds this negotiation part over deadline period different than contract law version? and also what is transfer orf risk actually? not the deadline?

Professor clarification:

Warranty law does not add a negotiation requirement. It adds a cure stage.

```text
General Section 323 route:
breach -> reasonable deadline to perform -> no proper performance -> revocation

Warranty route:
defect at transfer of risk -> buyer demands cure
-> repair/replacement question under Section 439
-> cure fails/refused/unreasonable/deadline expires
-> revocation/reduction/damages
```

Transfer of risk is not the deadline. It is the timing checkpoint for whether the defect belongs to the seller's warranty responsibility.

Usually:

```text
transfer of risk = handover / delivery under Section 446 BGB
```

It asks:

```text
Was the thing already defective at handover?
```

Examples:

- Laptop battery was internally defective at handover but discovered two days later: warranty can apply.
- Laptop was fine at handover but buyer spilled coffee later: no seller warranty defect.
- Washing machine delivered July 3, defect discovered July 5, cure deadline set for July 15: July 3 is transfer of risk; July 15 is the cure deadline.

Exam sentence:

```text
Transfer of risk determines whether the defect falls into the seller's warranty responsibility; the cure deadline determines whether the buyer may move from cure to secondary remedies.
```

## Refined Mental Models

- **Cure is the repair/replacement stage**, not compensation or reduction.
- **Disproportion limits the selected cure type**, but does not automatically eliminate all cure.
- **Disagreement is not failed cure** unless the seller refuses all meaningful cure or the reasonable cure route fails.
- **Warranty revocation is special purchase-law revocation**, not rescission.
- **Transfer of risk is the defect-timing checkpoint**, not the remedy deadline.
