## Recall Prompts And Raw Answers

### Case 1: Price Tag Laptop

Prompt: A customer sees a laptop on a shop shelf with a price tag of EUR 900, brings it to the cashier, and says: "I'll take this." The cashier replies: "No, that price tag was wrong; it is EUR 1,200." Questions: Was the shelf display an offer or `invitatio ad offerendum`? What was the customer's legal act? Which elements must an offer contain? Why does objective recipient horizon matter here?

User answer: "Violation of invitatio ad offerendum. It is basically an invitation to make an offer from 900 EUR and client sends an acceptance reply as it is intended to buy it from 900 EUR. However, the seller then justifies as the price is wrong and actual price is 1200 EUR. It is actually morally and legally violating the consumer rights. invitatio ad offerendum. declaration of intent. section 150? no, counter offer has been made by seller in essence"

Feedback: The shelf display is `invitatio ad offerendum`, but the customer's sentence is an offer, not acceptance. The key binding-offer anchor is Section 145 BGB, not Section 150 II BGB. No contract at EUR 900 because the cashier did not accept.

Which BGB anchor helps with email/message effectiveness?

User answer: "An online store shows a watch for EUR 300. once you click 'Place binding order.' once the customer receives 'Your order has been shipped.' the seller expects the offer against their Invitatio Ad Offerendum, only acceptance can be done against the offer. section 130"

Feedback: Correct. Add precision that the shipping email is acceptance when it reaches the customer under Section 130 BGB.

### Case 3: Modified Acceptance

Prompt: Supplier offers 100 kg coffee beans for EUR 8/kg by Friday. Cafe replies Thursday: "Agreed, but only for EUR 7.50/kg." Supplier does not answer. Questions: offer or invitation? definite elements? acceptance? key BGB anchor? contract formed?

User answer: "an offer. the offer contains the price, good and covers both parties. no, it is a counter offer. section 150. no, there is no acceptance yet"

Feedback: Correct. Use exact anchor Section 150 II BGB. Friday deadline also relates to Section 148 BGB, but modified acceptance is the main issue.

### Case 4: Ambiguous Message

Prompt: Seller texts: "I can sell you the oak table for EUR 400." Buyer replies: "Sounds good. I'll think about pickup." Seller claims acceptance; buyer says only interest. Questions: legal issue? offer? acceptance? interpretation anchors? objective recipient horizon?

User answer: "Wrong interpreation of declaration of intent of consumer by seller. yes, it contains the good and price with obivous parties. it contains ambigous intention. sections 133 and 157 BGB. It can be asked 3rd parties how they interpret this legal consequence"

Feedback: Good anchor selection. Correction: objective recipient horizon does not literally ask third parties; it asks how a reasonable recipient in the seller's position would understand the statement in context. Likely no acceptance.

### Case 5: Late Acceptance

### Case 6: Revocation Timing

Prompt: Seller emails at 09:00: "I sell you my camera for EUR 500." At 09:05 seller emails: "Ignore my previous email. I no longer want to sell." Buyer opens both at 10:00. Questions: offer? revocation? anchor? if both emails reach before reading, is offer effective? contract formed?

User answer: "first sentence since contains the price and good. second sentence, it clearly states the declararion of intent for not selling. section 146? yes, reception occurs as soon as the emails reaches to consumers ends. no, the offer has been revoked by seller"

Feedback: Correct conclusion, wrong anchor. Use Section 130 BGB. If revocation reaches before or at the same time, the offer does not become effective. Receipt does not require actual reading.

### Case 7: Valid Contract, But Maybe Void

### Case 8: Exploitative Price

### Case 9: Missing Form

Prompt: Private person sends WhatsApp: "I guarantee my friend's bank loan of EUR 20,000 if he cannot pay." Bank replies accepted. Friend defaults. Questions: offer and acceptance? validity problem? missing-form anchor? guarantee-specific anchor? can bank rely on WhatsApp guarantee?

### Case 10: Terms And Conditions

Prompt: Gym contract pre-written terms say: "The gym is not liable for any injury, even if caused intentionally by gym staff." Customer signs; staff intentionally injures customer. Questions: offer and acceptance? validity/control problem? standard terms anchor? intentional-liability anchor? can gym rely on clause?

User answer: "yes, both parties agreed over the offer with acceptance. there is a bad bargain chase regarding the client accepted the offer even contains the inteninoal injuries. I sense the section 138 BGB or 276 BGB suitable for that. section 276 BGB. no , its a violation of lAW"

Feedback: Main issue is standard terms control and mandatory liability limits, not bad bargain. Use Sections 305 ff. BGB and Section 276 III BGB. The clause is invalid/ineffective; the gym cannot rely on it.

### Capstone Case

Prompt: Furniture store website lists "Oak desk, EUR 700, delivery in Munich." Customer clicks "Place binding order." Automatic email says receipt is not acceptance. Next day store emails: "We accept, but only for EUR 750 because the EUR 700 price was a mistake." Standard terms say: "We are never liable for any damage, including intentional damage by our employees." Questions: Which step is `invitatio ad offerendum`? Which step is customer offer? Is "accept, but only EUR 750" acceptance? Modified acceptance anchor? Which clause creates standard terms/liability problem? Which anchors apply? Contract at EUR 700 or EUR 750?

User answer: "1- 'Oak desk, EUR 700, delivery in Munich.' 2-'Place binding order.' 3-no, its counter-offer to the client, no acceptance is cleared. 4-section 150 II BGB 5-Section 276 III BGB 6-SECTION 305 ff. BGB 7-No, both prices did not reached to an aggreement with offer acceptance"

Feedback: Pass. Correction: the problematic clause is the wording in the standard terms; the anchors are Sections 305 ff. BGB and 276 III BGB. No contract at EUR 700; no contract at EUR 750 unless the customer accepts the store's counteroffer.

## Refined Mental Model

Use this case-solving sequence:

1. Classify the first communication: `invitatio ad offerendum` or offer.
2. If offer, check definiteness: parties, object/service, price or determinable core terms.
3. Check acceptance: mirror image, timely, and effective upon receipt where relevant.
4. If the reply changes terms, apply Section 150 II BGB.
5. If timing matters, use Sections 148 and 146 BGB; if receipt/revocation matters, use Section 130 BGB.
6. If wording is ambiguous, use Sections 133 and 157 BGB with objective recipient horizon.
7. After formation, separately test validity: Section 125 for form, Section 134 for statutory prohibition, Section 138 for public-policy/usury, Sections 305 ff. and Section 276 III for standard terms and intentional-liability exclusions.

## Weak Spots

| Weak Spot | Quality | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Treating `invitatio ad offerendum` as a violated right | yellow | It is a classification of a communication, not a consumer-right violation. |
| Confusing customer offer with acceptance in shop/online cases | yellow | Product displays invite offers; checkout/cashier statement is usually the customer offer. |
| Wrong statutory anchor for binding offer or revocation timing | yellow | Use Section 145 BGB for binding offer; Section 130 BGB for receipt/revocation timing. |
| Objective recipient horizon wording | yellow | Ask how a reasonable recipient in that position understands the declaration, not literal third-party polling. |
| Legal consequence vocabulary | yellow | Use `void`, `ineffective`, `no timely acceptance`, and `counteroffer` precisely. |
| Clause issue vs whole-contract issue | yellow | Standard terms/liability clauses are controlled under Sections 305 ff. and Section 276 III BGB; not every unfair clause is Section 138 BGB. |
