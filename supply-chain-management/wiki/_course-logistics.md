## Exam Information

Source: `supply-chain-management/raw/01 Examination Rules.pdf`

Course label in source: Production and Logistics (HN), Exam Rules and Information, David Wuttke, 2023-04-12.

Exam rules:

- Exam date: **2026-07-20** (user-provided on 2026-06-13).
- Duration: 2 hours.
- Format: onsite only.
- Platform: Moodle examination page.
- Submission: all answers must be submitted in Moodle.
- Laptop: students must bring laptops to the exam.
- Exam type: closed-book.
- Prohibited: course material, software such as Excel, artificial intelligence, and access to the Moodle course page.
- Allowed browser use: only to access the Moodle examination page.
- Allowed calculator: non-programmable calculator.
- Allowed cheat sheet: one A4 page, handwritten with a pen, one side only. No printouts.
- Paper: clean paper sheets are provided during the exam; students must not bring other paper besides the one-page cheat sheet.
- Total points: 60.
- Passing threshold: 30 points.
- Scope: any topic and content from lecture and Moodle course page may be included. No specific content is excluded.
- Timing statement: the exam is intended to be reasonable so that 2 hours are sufficient.

Question types:

| Type | Description | Points |
|---|---|---:|
| A | Open-ended, for example numerical calculation | 2 |
| B | Multiple-selection questions where several options may be selected | 1 |

- Because sample exams ask for short explanations and quantitative recommendations, practice should require both formula setup and managerial interpretation.

## Literature And Preparation Guidance

Source: user-provided Moodle literature text, 2026-05-14.

General guidance:

- For most of the course, attending lectures actively and using the comprehensive lecture slide decks is sufficient.
- For each of the three case discussions, students are expected to prepare by studying the case upfront at home.
- Some chapters are expected to be read upfront when noted in daily descriptions.
- Complementary literature can be used to deepen knowledge or close gaps.

Cases:

- Available on Moodle for download.
- There are three case discussions in the course.
- Case preparation should be treated as mandatory when a case is assigned.

Books:

- Cachon and Terwiesch: *Matching Supply with Demand: An Introduction to Operations Management*.
- Chopra and Meindl: *Supply Chain Management: Strategy, Planning, and Operation, Global Edition*.
- Simchi-Levi, Chen, and Bramel: *The Logic of Logistics*.
- Ghiani, Laporte, and Musmanno: *Introduction to Logistics Management*.
- Heyman and Sobel: *Stochastic Models in Operations Research: Volume II*.
- Eliyahu Goldratt: *The Goal: A Process of Ongoing Improvement*.
- Womack and Jones: *Lean Thinking*.

Special recommendations:

- Goldratt's *The Goal* is highly recommended. It is a novel, easy to read, and useful for understanding the power of production and logistics.
- Womack and Jones' *Lean Thinking* is recommended for lean management and contains many examples and cases.

Study implication:

- Lecture slides remain the primary study artifact unless a case or chapter is explicitly assigned.
- Case discussion materials should be processed as high-priority because preparation is expected.
- The cheat sheet should gradually collect formulas, process-flow logic, inventory/queueing/forecasting decision rules, and case frameworks.
