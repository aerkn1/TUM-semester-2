This glossary follows the local lang-tutor word-tree idea: normalize each useful item, group it by part of speech, give article/plural or verb forms where useful, and include one short production prompt.

## Level Summary

- Main output domain: first meeting, classroom introduction, contact information.
- Must-produce items: greetings, `du`/`Sie` questions, `heißen`, `kommen`, `wohnen`, `sprechen`, `sein`, numbers, alphabet repair phrases.
- Main trap: register and verb-form mismatch, especially `du sprichst` and `Sie sind`.

## Fixed Phrases And Chunks

### Hallo!
- Meaning: hello; informal greeting.
- Use: classmates, friends, informal classroom pair work.
- Example: `Hallo, ich heiße Arda.`
- Pitfall: do not use it as the safest formal opening with an unknown administrator.
- Flashcard prompt: Say hello informally and give your name.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1 from course greeting material.

### Guten Tag!
- Meaning: good day; polite general greeting.
- Use: formal or neutral adult situations.
- Example: `Guten Tag. Mein Name ist Arda Erkan.`
- Pitfall: do not pair it with casual `du` unless the context explicitly becomes informal.
- Flashcard prompt: Open a formal conversation.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### Guten Morgen!
- Meaning: good morning.
- Use: polite morning greeting.
- Example: `Guten Morgen, Frau Weber.`
- Pitfall: `Morgen` here is not "tomorrow".
- Flashcard prompt: Greet your teacher in the morning.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### Guten Abend!
- Meaning: good evening.
- Use: polite evening greeting.
- Example: `Guten Abend. Wie heißen Sie?`
- Pitfall: do not replace it with `Gute Nacht` when arriving.
- Flashcard prompt: Greet someone in the evening.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### Gute Nacht!
- Meaning: good night.
- Use: farewell before sleep or late departure.
- Example: `Gute Nacht!`
- Pitfall: not a general evening greeting.
- Flashcard prompt: Say good night.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### Tschüss!
- Meaning: bye; informal goodbye.
- Use: informal closing.
- Example: `Tschüss, bis bald!`
- Pitfall: avoid as default in formal settings.
- Flashcard prompt: Close an informal dialogue.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### Auf Wiedersehen!
- Meaning: goodbye; formal or polite.
- Use: official or polite closing.
- Example: `Auf Wiedersehen, Herr Hansen.`
- Pitfall: longer but safer than `Tschüss` in formal situations.
- Flashcard prompt: Close a formal dialogue.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### Wie geht's? / Wie geht es dir?
- Meaning: how are you? informal.
- Use: after greeting someone with `du`.
- Example: `Wie geht es dir? Danke, gut.`
- Pitfall: do not answer with your name; this asks condition.
- Flashcard prompt: Ask a classmate how they are.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### Wie geht es Ihnen?
- Meaning: how are you? formal.
- Use: polite `Sie` context.
- Example: `Wie geht es Ihnen? Danke, gut.`
- Pitfall: `Ihnen` belongs to formal address; do not mix with `du`.
- Flashcard prompt: Ask formally how someone is.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### Danke, gut. / Auch gut, danke. / Ganz gut.
- Meaning: thank you, good; also good; quite good.
- Use: short A1 answers to well-being questions.
- Example: `Danke, gut. Und dir?`
- Pitfall: `ganz gut` is weaker than `sehr gut`.
- Flashcard prompt: Give three short answers to `Wie geht's?`
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### Wie bitte?
- Meaning: pardon? what was that?
- Use: short repair phrase when you did not understand.
- Example: `Wie bitte? Noch einmal bitte.`
- Pitfall: more classroom-safe than blunt `Was?`
- Flashcard prompt: Ask for repetition.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### Noch einmal bitte.
- Meaning: one more time, please.
- Use: ask someone to repeat.
- Example: `Entschuldigung, noch einmal bitte.`
- Pitfall: include `bitte` for politeness.
- Flashcard prompt: Ask your partner to repeat their email.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### Bitte ein bisschen langsamer.
- Meaning: a little more slowly, please.
- Use: repair phrase for fast speech.
- Example: `Bitte ein bisschen langsamer.`
- Pitfall: say the whole phrase; `langsamer` alone can sound abrupt.
- Flashcard prompt: Ask someone to slow down.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

## Nouns And Noun Phrases

### der Name
- Meaning: name.
- Plural: `die Namen`.
- Useful forms: `Mein Name ist ...`; `Wie ist Ihr Name?`
- Example: `Mein Name ist Sandra Bakker.`
- Pitfall: `Name` is masculine: `der Name`.
- Flashcard prompt: Say "My name is ...".
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### die Sprache
- Meaning: language.
- Plural: `die Sprachen`.
- Useful forms: `Welche Sprachen sprichst du?`
- Example: `Ich spreche Türkisch, Englisch und Deutsch.`
- Pitfall: the question uses plural `Sprachen`.
- Flashcard prompt: Ask which languages someone speaks.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### das Land
- Meaning: country.
- Plural: `die Länder`.
- Example: `Deutschland ist ein Land.`
- Pitfall: umlaut in plural: `Länder`.
- Flashcard prompt: Name your country in German.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### die Stadt
- Meaning: city.
- Plural: `die Städte`.
- Example: `Ich wohne in München.`
- Pitfall: do not use `aus` when the question is current residence.
- Flashcard prompt: Say where you live.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### die Telefonnummer
- Meaning: phone number.
- Plural: `die Telefonnummern`.
- Example: `Wie ist deine Telefonnummer?`
- Pitfall: long numbers need chunking.
- Flashcard prompt: Ask for a phone number.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### die Handynummer
- Meaning: mobile phone number.
- Plural: `die Handynummern`.
- Example: `Wie ist deine Handynummer?`
- Pitfall: `Handy` in German means mobile phone, not "useful".
- Flashcard prompt: Ask for a mobile number.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### die E-Mail-Adresse
- Meaning: email address.
- Plural: `die E-Mail-Adressen`.
- Example: `Wie ist deine E-Mail-Adresse?`
- Pitfall: spell symbols clearly: `Punkt`, `Minus`, `Unterstrich`.
- Flashcard prompt: Ask for an email address.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### das Alphabet
- Meaning: alphabet.
- Plural: usually `die Alphabete`, but plural is not needed for this lesson.
- Example: `Kannst du das buchstabieren?`
- Pitfall: spelling skill matters more than the noun itself.
- Flashcard prompt: Spell your surname.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

## Verbs

### heißen
- Meaning: to be called.
- Forms: `ich heiße`, `du heißt`, `er/sie heißt`, `Sie heißen`.
- Example: `Wie heißt du? Ich heiße Arda.`
- Pitfall: `ß` appears in `heißen`, but `du heißt` also keeps the long-s sound.
- Flashcard prompt: Ask and answer a name question.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### sein
- Meaning: to be.
- Forms: `ich bin`, `du bist`, `er/sie ist`, `Sie sind`.
- Example: `Wer bist du? Ich bin Arda.`
- Pitfall: fully irregular; do not regularize it.
- Flashcard prompt: Conjugate `sein` for `ich`, `du`, `er/sie`, `Sie`.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### kommen
- Meaning: to come; in this lesson, to come from.
- Forms: `ich komme`, `du kommst`, `er/sie kommt`, `Sie kommen`.
- Example: `Ich komme aus der Türkei.`
- Pitfall: origin uses `aus`.
- Flashcard prompt: Say where you come from.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### wohnen
- Meaning: to live/reside.
- Forms: `ich wohne`, `du wohnst`, `er/sie wohnt`, `Sie wohnen`.
- Example: `Ich wohne in München.`
- Pitfall: current residence uses `in`, not `aus`.
- Flashcard prompt: Say where you live.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### sprechen
- Meaning: to speak.
- Forms: `ich spreche`, `du sprichst`, `er/sie spricht`, `Sie sprechen`.
- Example: `Welche Sprachen sprichst du?`
- Pitfall: stem change in `du sprichst` and `er/sie spricht`.
- Flashcard prompt: Say which languages you speak.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### lernen
- Meaning: to learn.
- Forms: `ich lerne`, `du lernst`, `er/sie lernt`, `Sie lernen`.
- Example: `Ich lerne Deutsch.`
- Pitfall: do not use `studieren` for ordinary language learning here.
- Flashcard prompt: Say that you are learning German.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

### buchstabieren
- Meaning: to spell aloud.
- Forms: `ich buchstabiere`, `du buchstabierst`, `er/sie buchstabiert`, `Sie buchstabieren`.
- Example: `Kannst du das buchstabieren?`
- Pitfall: long verb; practice pronunciation in chunks.
- Flashcard prompt: Ask someone to spell their name.
- CEFR note: inferred A1.1.

## Pronouns And Question Words

| Item | Type | Meaning | Example | Pitfall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ich` | pronoun | I | `Ich heiße Arda.` | lower-case unless sentence-initial |
| `du` | pronoun | you informal | `Wo wohnst du?` | needs informal verb forms |
| `Sie` | pronoun | you formal | `Wie heißen Sie?` | capitalized in formal address |
| `er` | pronoun | he | `Er wohnt in München.` | use for masculine person/noun |
| `sie` | pronoun | she | `Sie kommt aus Japan.` | lower-case `sie` can mean she/they |
| `wer` | question word | who | `Wer bist du?` | people, not things |
| `wie` | question word | how/what in name phrase | `Wie heißt du?` | name question uses `wie`, not `was` |
| `wo` | question word | where | `Wo wohnst du?` | location |
| `woher` | question word | where from | `Woher kommst du?` | origin |
| `welche` | question word | which | `Welche Sprachen sprichst du?` | match the plural phrase |

## Numbers And Contact Symbols

| Item | Meaning | Example | Pitfall |
|---|---|---|---|
| `null` | 0 | phone numbers | not English "zero" in German output |
| `eins` | 1 | `eins, zwei, drei` | often becomes `ein` before nouns |
| `zwei` | 2 | number sequence | pronounce clearly, sometimes `zwo` in phone contexts |
| `drei` | 3 | number sequence | distinguish from `frei` |
| `@` / `at` | email at-symbol | `name at beispiel Punkt de` | course source says "at" |
| `Punkt` | dot | email address | use for `.` |
| `Minus` | hyphen | email address | use for `-` |
| `Unterstrich` | underscore | email address | use for `_` |
