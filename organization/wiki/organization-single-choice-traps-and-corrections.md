# Organization Single-Choice Traps And Corrections

Created: 2026-08-03

Use this sheet for rapid single-choice exam preparation. It is a cross-session Organization exam aid and does not mark any topic `First Pass` as completed.

## Single-Choice Rule

If two answers look true, ask:

```text
Which option matches the exact theory, level of analysis, and case wording?
```

Be suspicious of options using:

```text
always, only, automatically, best in general, purely, no structure, neutral, objective
```

The best answer often says:

```text
It depends on fit, enactment, boundary conditions, and the specific organizing problem.
```

The wrong answer often says:

```text
This structure, tool, method, or theory always leads to this outcome.
```

## Core Traps And Corrections

| Trap option | Correction |
|---|---|
| An organization must be legally registered. | No. Apply the four criteria: social entity, goal-directed, deliberately structured, environment-linked. |
| A hierarchy is required for an organization. | No. Loose movements can still be organization-like if coordination and structure exist. |
| Organizations have one clear goal. | No. Goals can be multiple, conflicting, or changing. |
| Formal structure explains what really happens. | No. Compare formal design with organizing in practice. |
| Rules prevent errors automatically. | No. Rules work only if enacted, interpreted, and escalated. |
| Procedures and rules are the same. | No. Rule = allowed, required, or prohibited; procedure = step sequence. |
| Matrix structure is generally superior. | No. It fits dual demands but creates conflict and dual authority. |
| Functional structure is best for product diversity. | Usually no. Divisional structure fits product, region, or customer diversity better. |
| Environment means market competition only. | No. Include task environment and global environment. |
| Suppliers are simply outside the organization. | Not necessarily. Boundaries are analytical and shifting. |
| Uncertainty means environmental change only. | No. Uncertainty = environmental change x environmental complexity. |
| Contingency theory explains population survival. | No. Contingency theory = structure-environment fit. Population ecology = selection and retention of forms. |
| Resource dependence is just uncertainty. | No. It is dependence caused by need, scarcity, and lack of substitutes. |
| Mechanistic structures fit dynamic environments. | Usually no. Dynamic or complex environments require more organic forms. |
| Strategy is only top management planning. | No. Strategy can emerge through practice, routines, interpretation, and middle-manager action. |
| Mission, vision, purpose, and goals are interchangeable. | No. Mission = what we do; vision = future image; purpose = why we exist; goals = concrete targets. |
| Diversification creates value because the firm grows. | No. Apply Porter's three tests: attractiveness, cost-of-entry, better-off. |
| Ethics equals legality. | No. Legal compliance is the minimum; stakeholder effects can go beyond law. |
| Technology means machines only. | No. Technology includes tools, methods, procedures, skills, and knowledge. |
| Same technology has the same effect everywhere. | No. Effects depend on enactment, technological frames, routines, and power. |
| Perrow asks whether technology is advanced. | No. Perrow classifies work by task variability x task analyzability. |
| AI automatically replaces humans. | No. Analyze automation, augmentation, and adjacent human work. |
| AI is neutral or objective because it is data-driven. | No. Check bias, transparency, accountability, privacy, and governance. |
| Algorithmic dashboards are just information tools. | No. They can become control instruments. |
| Automation and augmentation are clean opposites. | No. They are interdependent; automation can create new human tasks, and augmentation can prepare later automation. |
| Informal organization just means unofficial behavior. | Too shallow. It includes norms, culture, power, knowledge, conflict, and workarounds. |
| Strong culture is always good. | No. It can support coordination but also suppress criticism and learning. |
| Tacit knowledge means secret information. | No. It means hard-to-articulate know-how learned through practice. |
| Power equals formal authority. | No. Power also comes from resources, centrality, expertise, uncertainty coping, and non-substitutability. |
| Conflict is always bad. | No. Moderate task conflict can improve performance; too little or too much conflict is harmful. |
| Change means announcing a new structure or tool. | No. Real change includes adoption, routines, identity, politics, capability, and reinforcement. |
| Kotter is enough if the steps are listed. | No. Steps must be linked to case facts and implementation barriers. |
| Bridges is about external change. | No. Bridges focuses on internal psychological transition. |
| SCARF is a full change roadmap. | No. SCARF is a social threat/reward lens, not a complete implementation plan. |
| Skills architecture is the same as job architecture. | No. Job architecture classifies roles and levels; skill architecture classifies abilities and proficiency. |
| Skill databases automatically improve fairness. | No. They can also create surveillance, bias, opacity, and contestability problems. |
| New organizational forms are new because they are trendy. | No. Apply the four-problem canvas: task division, task allocation, reward provision, information provision. |
| Self-management means no structure. | No. Authority is structured differently. |
| Holacracy removes formalization. | No. It reduces managerial hierarchy but increases formal governance. |
| Network organizations have no hierarchy or control. | No. They rely on contracts, trust, governance, boundary roles, and mutual adjustment. |
| Scrum is just a project-management tool. | No. It reshapes roles, events, artifacts, coordination, visibility, and learning. |
| Design thinking means brainstorming. | No. It includes user research, problem definition, prototyping, testing, and iteration. |
| OKR is just a KPI list. | No. OKR links strategic intent to measurable progress and learning. |
| Moonshots are good for operational reliability. | No. Use roofshots for binding operational, compliance, or reliability targets. |

## Lens Selection Traps

| If the question asks about... | Pick this lens | Avoid this wrong lens |
|---|---|---|
| Whether something is an organization | Four definitional criteria | Legal form only |
| Why official rules did not work | Formal design plus enactment | Individual blame only |
| Structure under stable vs turbulent conditions | Contingency theory | Population ecology |
| Dependence on suppliers, partners, or scarce resources | Resource dependence theory | Generic uncertainty |
| Survival of organizational forms over time | Population ecology | Managerial fit choice only |
| AI changing tasks and roles | Automation, augmentation, expertise, governance | Replacement-only logic |
| Dashboard, scoring, ranking, or monitoring | Algorithmic control | Neutral information system |
| Culture or invisible assumptions | Schein: artifacts, espoused values, basic assumptions | One visible artifact only |
| Knowledge transfer | Tacit/explicit knowledge and SECI | Data or information only |
| Change resistance | ADKAR, Bridges, SCARF, power, identity | Irrational employee resistance |
| New organizational forms | Four-problem novelty canvas | Trend label only |
| Scrum, design thinking, or OKR | Coordination, information, motivation, decision logic | Tool or method label only |

## Fast Drill Prompts

Answer these closed-book before checking the session notes.

1. Why can Fridays for Future be considered organization-like even without a classic hierarchy?
2. Why is `rules exist` not enough to conclude that risk is controlled?
3. When does a functional structure become weaker than a divisional structure?
4. How do environmental change and complexity jointly create uncertainty?
5. What is the difference between contingency theory, resource dependence, and population ecology?
6. Why can the same technology produce different organizational effects in two firms?
7. Why is AI neither automatically automation nor automatically augmentation?
8. How can a dashboard become algorithmic control?
9. Why can strong culture become dangerous?
10. Why is tacit knowledge not the same as secret information?
11. Which change framework would you use for individual adoption bottlenecks, and why?
12. Why is holacracy not the same as having no structure?
13. How do Scrum, design thinking, and OKR differ?
14. Why are moonshot OKRs inappropriate for operational reliability?
