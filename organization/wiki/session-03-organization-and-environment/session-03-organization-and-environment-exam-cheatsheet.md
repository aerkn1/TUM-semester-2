# Session 03: Organization And Environment - Exam Cheat Sheet

Source note: [session-03-organization-and-environment.md](session-03-organization-and-environment.md)

Created: 2026-07-15

Use this sheet for rapid exam recall. It does not replace the full note and does not mark `First Pass` as completed.

## Core Exam Move

```text
Draw boundary -> classify environment/interdependence -> diagnose uncertainty
-> choose theory -> recommend fit or boundary response.
```

## Boundary Sentence

```text
Organizational boundaries are analytical distinctions that are socially produced, maintained, multiple, and shifting.
```

Do not rely only on employment, ownership, membership, or legal status. In outsourcing, platforms, partnerships, and ecosystems, actors can be outside the legal organization but inside the task system.

## Environment Router

| Concept | Meaning | Case examples |
|---|---|---|
| Task environment | Direct actors/forces linked to task accomplishment | suppliers, buyers, collaborators, banks, direct regulators |
| Global environment | Broad conditions shaping action | technology, political-legal, socio-cultural, ecological, macroeconomic |
| Pooled interdependence | Units contribute separately to a larger whole/share resources | branches using same brand/IT |
| Sequential interdependence | One output becomes another input | sales -> engineering; production line |
| Reciprocal interdependence | Units mutually adjust in ongoing exchange | hospital team; product development |

Higher interdependence usually requires richer coordination.

## Uncertainty Matrix

Uncertainty depends on:

```text
environmental change x environmental complexity
```

| Change | Complexity | Uncertainty | Design implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| stable | simple | low | mechanistic can work |
| stable | complex | low-moderate | specialized sensing and coordination |
| unstable | simple | high-moderate | flexible response to change |
| unstable | complex | high | organic structure, boundary spanning, learning |

## Theory Comparison

| Theory | Core claim | Level | Best exam use | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Contingency theory | structure must fit environment; no one best way | firm/design | mechanistic vs organic fit | not population-level selection |
| Resource dependence | dependence rises with need and scarcity; falls with substitutes | interorganizational exchange | supplier/resource vulnerability | not just uncertainty in general |
| Population ecology | environments select organizational forms through variation, selection, retention | population | why forms survive/fail | not primarily managerial choice |

Mechanistic vs organic:

```text
Mechanistic = stable, specialized, top-down, vertical communication.
Organic = dynamic, flexible, mutual adaptation, horizontal communication.
```

## Managing The Environment

| Response | What it does |
|---|---|
| Boundary spanning | Connects organization and environment through roles, units, alliances. |
| Public relations/lobbying | Attempts to shape relevant publics and political-legal conditions. |
| Differentiation | Creates autonomous units for different environmental demands. |
| Sensing | Uses forecasting, planning, and bottom-up feedback to detect change. |

Exam-safe insight:

```text
Organizations adapt to environments, but they can also try to reshape parts of the environment.
```

## BP Deepwater Horizon Part 2 Route

Question:

```text
How did interorganizational dependencies and information asymmetries weaken uncertainty absorption?
```

Case anchors:

- Transocean: rig, crew, offshore interpretation.
- Halliburton: cementing, modeling, mudlogging, technical information.
- Schlumberger: cement bond log diagnostic option.
- Weatherford: centralizer supply.
- Regulators: approvals, oversight, institutional expectations.

Interpretation:

```text
Macondo was not only BP internal control failure. It was an interorganizational system failure involving authority, expertise, diagnostics, materials, equipment, data visibility, and oversight under uncertainty.
```

## Trap Corrections

| Trap | Correction |
|---|---|
| "Environment = market competition." | Include task and global environments. |
| "Supplier is simply outside." | Explain boundary logic and dependence. |
| "Uncertainty is high." | Name change and complexity. |
| Confusing contingency and ecology | Contingency = fit choice; ecology = population selection. |

## Last Line

```text
Environment questions are fit questions: what uncertainty and dependence exist, and what structure or boundary practice absorbs them?
```
